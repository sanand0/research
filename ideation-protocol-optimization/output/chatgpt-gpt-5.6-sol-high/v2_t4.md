## 1. BAN: the obvious mode

These seven mechanisms are excluded, including cosmetic variations:

1. **Audiobook variants** — narration, voice acting, soundscapes, adjustable playback.
2. **Branching stories** — choices, alternate endings, personalized plot paths.
3. **Compression** — summaries, highlights, “key ideas,” layered abridgments.
4. **Social reading** — book clubs, shared annotations, crowd commentary.
5. **Conversational books** — AI tutors, character chats, question-answer interfaces.
6. **Simulated immersion** — augmented reality, virtual reality, cinematic environments.
7. **Gamification** — points, streaks, achievements, quests, progress rewards.

The candidates below do not depend on narration, branching, abridgment, social interaction, conversation, simulation, or rewards.

## 2. DIVERGE

### Frame the space

A reading format can vary independently along six dimensions:

| Dimension              | Possible variations                                                                              |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Readable unit**      | Sentence, grammatical role, claim, consequence, revision, physical layer                         |
| **Sequencing rule**    | Author order, delayed causality, reader commitment, physiological state, environmental encounter |
| **Persistence**        | Permanent, fading, destructible, transforming, recoverable only later                            |
| **Reader identity**    | Present self, future self, embodied self, reconstructing self                                    |
| **Required operation** | Observe, predict, infer, manipulate, wait, physically alter                                      |
| **Source of meaning**  | Stable text, missing text, changes between versions, spatial adjacency, temporal separation      |

### Two borrowed domains

#### Domain A: Cryptographic protocols

Atomic structural rules:

1. **Commit–reveal:** a party must irreversibly commit before seeing later information.
2. **Secret sharing:** no single fragment is sufficient; a quorum of fragments reconstructs the whole.
3. **Zero-knowledge proof:** understanding can be demonstrated without reproducing the underlying information.

#### Domain B: Ecological succession

Atomic structural rules:

1. **Pioneer layers modify the substrate**, enabling later layers that could not previously exist.
2. **Disturbance destroys local structure but creates new interpretive niches.**
3. **The system’s meaning lies in interactions across time**, not in any single snapshot.

The borrowed-rule candidates below require these structures; removing the borrowed rule would collapse the format.

### Merge by causal mechanism

The overlaps consolidate as follows:

* **Commit–Reveal Edition + Evidence Escrow** → **Commit–Reveal Book**
  Both make the reader expose a belief before receiving evidence.

* **Succession Ink + Marginal Colonization** → **Succession Palimpsest**
  Both make one textual population transform the substrate for the next.

* **Absent-Core Manuscript + Consequence-Only Narrative** → **Absent Core**
  Both communicate through evidence surrounding text that is never available.

* **Contradiction Gradient + Semantic Drift Ledger** → **Semantic Drift Edition**
  Both encode meaning in mutation rather than stable statements.

After removing weaker variants and mechanisms too close to one another, eight survivors remain:

1. Commit–Reveal Book
2. Future-Self Secret Share
3. Succession Palimpsest
4. Pulse-Gated Prose
5. Grammatical Skeleton
6. Absent Core
7. Folded-Causality Codex
8. Semantic Drift Edition

## 3. CONVERGE

### Survivor critiques

| Survivor                     | Assumption that must hold                                                                             | Cheapest falsifying observation                                                                                                                   | Why obvious ideas miss it                                                                             |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Commit–Reveal Book**       | Committing to an interpretation before evidence produces more active, calibrated reading.             | Readers forced to commit show no improvement in recall, belief revision, or recognition of surprise over an ordinary edition.                     | Conventional formats optimize information delivery, not the order in which beliefs become reversible. |
| **Future-Self Secret Share** | Incomplete views separated by time produce synthesis rather than mere frustration.                    | Readers receiving three partial views form no better final model than readers receiving the complete text at once, while dropping out more often. | Serialization normally divides quantity; this divides the conceptual keys needed for comprehension.   |
| **Succession Palimpsest**    | Irreversible physical transformation makes interpretation cumulative and consequential.               | Readers treat the transformations as a gimmick and cannot articulate relationships between successive layers.                                     | Digital reading assumes perfect reversibility: every state can always be revisited unchanged.         |
| **Pulse-Gated Prose**        | The useful unit of pacing is the reader’s cognitive or physiological transition, not elapsed seconds. | State-gated pauses perform no better than matched fixed pauses on comprehension and abandonment.                                                  | Existing pacing controls regulate words per minute, treating all moments of attention as equivalent.  |
| **Grammatical Skeleton**     | Separating linguistic roles exposes structures normally hidden by fluent reading.                     | Readers reconstruct little and understand less than readers seeing complete sentences, even after brief adaptation.                               | Most format experiments alter chapter order or media, not the internal channels of language.          |
| **Absent Core**              | A book can be understood from its traces without ever displaying its central text.                    | Different readers reconstruct unrelated cores without identifying common invariants.                                                              | Normal formats assume the object being read must be materially present.                               |
| **Folded-Causality Codex**   | Physical adjacency can express relationships more effectively than sequential order.                  | Readers’ causal maps are no better after manipulating the codex than after reading an ordinary diagrammed edition.                                | The rectangular page is treated as a neutral container rather than a semantic operator.               |
| **Semantic Drift Edition**   | A sequence of small mutations can communicate an idea unavailable in any single version.              | Readers remember isolated phrases but cannot infer the direction, attractor, or concept encoded by the changes.                                   | Publishing hides revision history and presents stability as a prerequisite for meaning.               |

### Pairwise knockout

#### Practical bracket

**Commit–Reveal Book vs. Grammatical Skeleton**
**Winner: Commit–Reveal Book.** It can work with fiction, essays, textbooks, and journalism, whereas grammatical separation is powerful but narrower and demands substantial reader retraining.

**Pulse-Gated Prose vs. Folded-Causality Codex**
**Winner: Pulse-Gated Prose.** Folded causality offers a distinctive print experience, but state-sensitive pacing could alter reading across existing digital devices and many genres.

**Commit–Reveal Book vs. Pulse-Gated Prose**
**Winner: Commit–Reveal Book.** Pulse gating changes when information arrives. Commit–reveal changes the epistemic act itself: readers must discover not only what the author thinks, but what they themselves believed before being persuaded.

#### Wildcard bracket

**Future-Self Secret Share vs. Absent Core**
**Winner: Future-Self Secret Share.** Both require reconstruction, but the secret-share mechanism creates a repeatable format rather than primarily an experimental literary technique.

**Succession Palimpsest vs. Semantic Drift Edition**
**Winner: Succession Palimpsest.** Semantic drift changes the text’s states; succession makes reading itself cause those state changes.

**Future-Self Secret Share vs. Succession Palimpsest**
**Winner: Succession Palimpsest.** The secret share radically changes distribution over time. The palimpsest goes further by making interpretation materially alter the book that remains available.

## 4. Recommendations

### Best practical idea: Commit–Reveal Book

A chapter begins with a claim, puzzle, scene, or disputed event. Before seeing the explanation, evidence, or resolution, the reader must record:

* what they think is happening;
* what evidence they expect;
* what would change their mind.

The commitment is sealed. The reader then receives the text and finally sees their earlier position beside the author’s evidence.

**Non-obvious insight:** Reading becomes more meaningful when the reader’s prior interpretation is made temporarily irreversible. Surprise, persuasion, and learning become visible differences between two recorded mental states.

**First cheap test:** Create a six-page essay as a simple form. Half the readers must commit predictions and falsifiers before opening each evidence section; half read normally. Compare delayed recall, calibration, reported surprise, and how accurately each group identifies what changed its mind.

### Practical runner-up: Pulse-Gated Prose

Text appears in clauses rather than scrolling continuously. The next clause becomes available only after a state transition—for example, a slow exhale, a reduction in reading regressions, or a short period without input.

The pauses are not rewards or locks. They are part of the punctuation system.

**Non-obvious insight:** A sentence may be grammatically complete before the reader is cognitively ready for the next one. Current formats can detect page position but not completion of attention.

**First cheap test:** Build two versions of an 800-word essay. One uses fixed four-second pauses; the other requires the reader to hold a key through a slow exhale before consequential clauses appear. Compare comprehension and abandonment. This cheaply tests the central pacing hypothesis before adding sensors.

### Best wildcard: Succession Palimpsest

The book contains successive textual ecosystems. Reading one layer physically transforms it:

* rubbing a page exposes a second voice while erasing parts of the first;
* body heat reveals a counterargument that vanishes as the page cools;
* removing a translucent sentence layer permanently changes which words connect;
* annotations from an earlier layer become the body text of the next.

There is no untouched original to return to. The reader’s copy develops a history.

**Non-obvious insight:** Reading need not merely alter the reader. It can alter the future readability of the book. Interpretation becomes an ecological succession in which each textual layer creates the conditions for its successor.

**First cheap test:** Produce an eight-page handmade edition using tracing paper, removable labels, graphite rubbings, and cut-outs. Give copies to ten readers. Kill the concept if they cannot explain how an earlier act changed the meaning available later.

### Wildcard runner-up: Future-Self Secret Share

The book is divided into three or more complementary encodings delivered to the same reader at different times. Each installment is coherent but systematically insufficient:

* one contains events without motives;
* another contains motives without identities;
* the last contains relationships without chronology.

Only the reader’s memories of all installments reconstruct the book. Reopening earlier installments does not solve it; they were designed to function as shares, not chapters.

**Non-obvious insight:** A single person at different moments can behave like multiple parties in a cryptographic protocol. Reading can require agreement among past, present, and future versions of the reader.

**First cheap test:** Create three five-minute fragments delivered three days apart. Compare them with a control group receiving the same material together. Continue only if delayed-share readers produce more integrated causal models without unacceptable dropout.
