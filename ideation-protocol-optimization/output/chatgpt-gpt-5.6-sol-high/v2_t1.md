# 1. BAN

These are the ideas a smart generalist would produce almost immediately. They are banned, including renamed or lightly disguised versions.

1. **Streaks, points, badges, levels, rewards, leaderboards, virtual pets.**
2. **Push notifications, reminders, calendar nudges, “best time” messaging.**
3. **More content:** larger libraries, shorter sessions, celebrity teachers, themed packs.
4. **Generic personalization:** AI coaches, recommended sessions, adaptive plans.
5. **Social accountability:** buddies, challenges, public commitments, community feeds.
6. **Friction reduction as the main idea:** easier onboarding, one-tap starts, fewer choices.
7. **Acquisition and pricing tactics:** referrals, discounts, free trials, paid campaigns.

The ideas below must create daily use through a different causal mechanism—not merely make meditation more attractive or remind users to do it.

# 2. DIVERGE

## Frame the space

Independent dimensions along which the solution can vary:

| Dimension                  | Possibilities                                                                                                                               |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Trigger                    | Internal curiosity, physical transition, digital action, work event, another user, forced waiting                                           |
| Primary value              | Calm, self-measurement, closure, readiness, boundary-setting, social presence                                                               |
| Temporal structure         | On demand, fixed departure, delayed reveal, paired sessions, event-triggered                                                                |
| Product role               | Destination, gateway, diagnostic instrument, infrastructure, record system                                                                  |
| Actor controlling adoption | Individual, employer, therapist, partner platform                                                                                           |
| Inversion                  | Abundance → scarcity; motivation → automatic context; private activity → anonymous synchrony; meditation as outcome → meditation as doorway |

## Initial candidates

These occupy combinations not covered by the banned mechanisms:

* **Personal Causality Lab:** users return to discover whether meditation measurably changes their cognition.
* **Tomorrow Envelope:** today’s session produces something inaccessible until tomorrow.
* **Chosen App Gate:** the first daily use of a user-selected distracting app passes through a brief meditation.
* **Post-Send Exhale:** a decompression protocol immediately after sending a high-stakes message.
* **Silent Lottery:** instant anonymous co-presence with another meditator, without profiles or communication.
* **Workday Closing Bell:** meditation performs the functional act of ending work.
* **Therapist Handoff Note:** sessions produce a compact longitudinal record for therapy or coaching.
* **Session-to-Focus Switch:** completing a session changes the device into a distraction-restricted work state.

## Borrowing from unrelated domains

### Domain A: Public transit

Atomic structural rules:

1. **Fixed departures convert vague intention into arrival behaviour.**
2. **Stations capture people at transitions they already must pass through.**
3. **A rider can join the system without selecting from a large catalogue.**

Candidates that require these rules:

* **Meditation Departures:** live sessions leave every ten minutes and cannot be rewound.
* **Meeting Vestibule:** meditation is the waiting room through which users enter recurring meetings.
* **Queue Calm SDK:** partner apps offer meditation during unavoidable waiting states.

“Zen Metro Map” was dropped because it borrowed transit aesthetics rather than transit mechanics.

### Domain B: Double-entry accounting

Atomic structural rules:

1. **Every consequential entry requires a corresponding counter-entry.**
2. **Unresolved items remain open until reconciliation.**
3. **An audit trail makes invisible changes inspectable.**

Candidates that require these rules:

* **Open-Loop Ledger:** stressful events remain open until paired with a short settling action.
* **Emotional Reconciliation:** users periodically close one unresolved event rather than browse meditation content.
* **Recovery Receipt:** every session records before/after state and the subsequent action taken.

“Calm Balance Sheet Badges” was dropped because it reduced accounting to gamified terminology.

# 3. CONVERGE

## Critique of each survivor

| Survivor                 | Assumption that must hold                                                       | Cheapest falsifying observation                                                                       | Why obvious ideas miss it                                                                                |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Personal Causality Lab   | People care enough about their own mental performance to measure it repeatedly  | Add a 20-second test before existing sessions; see whether users voluntarily repeat it for seven days | Most apps treat meditation as content, not as an intervention whose effect can be measured               |
| Tomorrow Relay           | An unfinished self-authored loop creates next-day curiosity                     | Let users record a ten-second message revealed only on next open; measure next-day return             | Retention work usually adds external reminders rather than intrinsic incompleteness                      |
| Ephemeral Departures     | Scarcity and synchrony outweigh the convenience of on-demand access             | Offer identical audio either on demand or at ten-minute departures                                    | Meditation libraries optimize choice, even though choice removes urgency                                 |
| Transition Gateway       | Users will accept a short pause when it sits inside an existing daily doorway   | Prototype a browser or mobile shortcut before the first meeting or selected app                       | Conventional approaches create a new habit instead of attaching to an unavoidable one                    |
| High-Stakes Buffer       | Emotional actions occur frequently enough to create repeat use                  | Test a share-sheet or browser extension after sending important messages                              | Meditation is generally positioned before stress or at scheduled times, not beside consequential actions |
| Queue Calm SDK           | Waiting states can become product usage without requiring a separate habit      | Place a two-minute session in one real partner queue and measure usage                                | Growth teams usually distribute marketing, not the product experience itself                             |
| Silent Lottery           | Anonymous presence is motivating even without accountability or conversation    | Show the number of silent participants and compare completion against solo sessions                   | Social meditation is normally built around profiles, friends or challenges                               |
| Open-Loop Reconciliation | Users want emotional closure enough to confront one open item daily             | Add a single “close one open loop” flow and inspect avoidance/drop-off                                | Content apps avoid asking users to name the actual event causing distress                                |
| Recovery Receipt         | Visible evidence of change increases return behaviour                           | Show before/after state and next action for one week; then remove it for a matched group              | Apps track minutes completed rather than whether the session altered anything                            |
| State-Change Utility     | Meditation becomes habitual when it performs a concrete device or work function | Compare meditation alone with meditation that activates focus mode or closes the workday              | Most products sell the internal experience, not an external consequence                                  |
| Calm Tile                | Physical placement can outperform digital reminders                             | Give twenty users NFC stickers for one recurrent location; count daily taps                           | Product teams assume the habit must originate on-screen                                                  |
| Therapist Handoff Note   | A meaningful subgroup meditates more when sessions improve professional support | Give users a one-page weekly summary and measure sharing and repeat use                               | Consumer apps rarely design meditation as input to a larger care relationship                            |

## Pairwise knockout

Criterion: **Which mechanism is most likely to materially increase daily active use for this app?**

### First round

* **Personal Causality Lab defeats Recovery Receipt.** Causal evidence gives users a reason to perform another measurement; a historical record can become passive.
* **Tomorrow Relay defeats Therapist Handoff Note.** The relay applies to nearly every user; therapist integration serves a narrower population.
* **Ephemeral Departures defeats Silent Lottery.** Fixed departures generate urgency without depending on successful matching or comfort with strangers.
* **Transition Gateway defeats Calm Tile.** Both attach meditation to a location or transition, but software gateways scale without hardware distribution.
* **High-Stakes Buffer defeats Open-Loop Reconciliation.** The triggering event is concrete and immediate; reconciliation requires users to initiate emotionally demanding reflection.
* **State-Change Utility defeats Queue Calm SDK.** It directly raises activity in the owned app, while SDK usage may depend on how DAU is defined and attributed.

### Second round

* **Personal Causality Lab defeats High-Stakes Buffer.** A daily baseline can apply every day; high-stakes actions may be intermittent.
* **Transition Gateway defeats State-Change Utility.** The gateway guarantees a trigger before meditation; state-change utility rewards users only after they have already decided to open the app.
* **Ephemeral Departures defeats Tomorrow Relay.** Departures can create urgency for new and returning users immediately; the relay requires participation on the previous day.

### Final comparisons

* **Transition Gateway defeats Personal Causality Lab.** Measurement still requires curiosity and voluntary initiation. A gateway inherits frequency from an existing daily behaviour.
* **Transition Gateway defeats Ephemeral Departures.** Daily doorways are more reliable than scheduled attendance and require less continuous live-programming operation.

Overall knockout winner: **Transition Gateway**.

# Recommendations

## Best practical idea 1: Transition Gateway

Attach a 30–60 second meditation to a transition users already cross daily:

* Before joining the first work meeting.
* Before opening a user-selected distracting app for the first time that day.
* Before entering a recurring focus block.
* At the transition from work mode to personal mode.

The meditation should not merely interrupt the action. It should complete the transition: join the meeting, unlock the selected app, change status, or activate focus mode.

**Non-obvious insight:** The app does not need to create a meditation habit. It needs to become the doorway to a habit that already exists.

**First cheap test:** Build a browser extension that intercepts the first calendar-meeting link each day, plays a 45-second silent breathing protocol, then opens the meeting. Compare fourteen-day active days against a control extension that opens meetings normally. Kill the idea if bypass rates remain high after three uses or if the gateway fails to increase active days without harming retention.

## Best practical idea 2: Personal Causality Lab

Make the app a daily instrument for answering: **“Does meditation actually work for me today?”**

A session begins with a short, repeatable measurement such as reaction-time variability, sustained attention, self-rated mental noise or voice steadiness. The app occasionally measures without meditation as a control. After several observations, it reports the user’s personal effect rather than generic wellness claims.

The user returns to inspect themselves; meditation becomes the experimental intervention.

**Non-obvious insight:** Self-knowledge can have higher daily pull than meditation content. People may not want another session, but they may want today’s reading and an updated answer about what changes their state.

**First cheap test:** Add a 20-second attention test before and after a two-minute session. Randomly omit the meditation on some days. Compare active days against the same meditation without measurement. Kill the idea if users stop performing the test before enough observations accumulate or if viewing the personal result does not predict subsequent return.

## Best wildcard: Ephemeral Departures

Replace the content catalogue with a transit system.

A session “departs” every ten minutes. Users see the next departure, the approximate number joining and its duration. Once it begins, participants join live; it cannot be rewound or browsed later. Different durations can depart on different schedules.

This is not a social challenge. There are no profiles, scores or obligations. The mechanism is punctuality, scarcity and anonymous synchrony.

**Non-obvious insight:** On-demand abundance may suppress daily use by making every session indefinitely postponable. A small amount of inconvenience can create stronger action than perfect availability.

**First cheap test:** Use the same three audio sessions in two conditions: ordinary on-demand access versus departures every ten minutes. Compare completed sessions, active days per user and abandonment caused by waiting. Kill the idea if departures merely delay usage or reduce total completion without increasing return frequency.
