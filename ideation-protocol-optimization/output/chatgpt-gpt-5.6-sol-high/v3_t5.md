## FRAME

**Binding constraint:** At peak times, a few intersections and curb zones cross a saturation threshold; stopping, turning, merging, parking search, and blocked junctions then create spillback that reduces the capacity of the entire network.

**The 30-second mode—banned:**

1. Build or widen roads.
2. Expand public transport.
3. Introduce congestion charges or higher parking prices.
4. Stagger working hours or encourage remote work.
5. Add cycling, walking, or micromobility infrastructure.
6. Promote carpooling or HOV lanes.
7. Install smarter traffic signals.

Also banned are thin disguises of these mechanisms: adding capacity elsewhere, subsidizing another mode, pricing scarce road space indirectly, shifting the same trips to adjacent hours, increasing vehicle occupancy, or merely optimizing conventional signal phases.

## DIVERGE

### Independent dimensions

* **Friction attacked:** curb dwell, parking search, turning conflicts, incidents, private-building queues, service trips.
* **Control mechanism:** admission, containment, batching, topology change, obligation, reservation, trip deletion.
* **Adopter:** city, building owner, major destination, fleet, neighborhood association, service provider.
* **Control unit:** vehicle, junction, curb bay, building, block, district.
* **Intervention point:** before departure, zone entry, intersection entry, destination arrival, blockage detection.
* **Time horizon:** software pilot, reversible street operation, building retrofit, permanent regulation.

### Borrowed domain 1: operating systems

Atomic rules:

* **Admission control:** do not accept new work when downstream capacity is unavailable.
* **Locality:** place frequently requested resources close to repeated demand.
* **Compaction:** consolidate fragmented free space so it becomes usable capacity.

### Borrowed domain 2: immune systems

Atomic rules:

* **Containment:** isolate a local overload before it propagates system-wide.
* **Memory:** recurring threats should trigger prepared responses rather than fresh diagnosis.
* **Mobile cleanup:** rapidly remove local obstructions before secondary damage accumulates.

### Candidate pool after merging causal overlaps

1. **Curb operating system—0.55:** Make dense commercial curbs reservation-only, impose vehicle-specific dwell limits, and automatically enforce overruns.
2. **Backpressure gates—0.18:** Permit entry into a junction or district only when sensors confirm sufficient downstream storage.
3. **Rapid-clearance pods—0.32:** Station small tow, repair, and spill-response units beside recurrent blockage locations rather than at central depots.
4. **Hotspot immune memory—0.12:** Automatically activate a pre-approved package of turn restrictions, enforcement, and access controls when a recurring congestion signature appears.
5. **Neighborhood service caches—0.14:** Store frequently delivered goods, tools, returns, and replacement parts inside neighborhoods to eliminate repeated cross-city service trips.
6. **Curb compaction—0.22:** Replace scattered loading permissions with continuous shared transfer bays that serve several buildings with fewer stopping maneuvers.
7. **Peak no-left-turn mesh—0.38:** Remove conflict-heavy turns across a connected area and route them through a small number of protected turning loops.
8. **Queue-neutral building code—0.16:** Require large buildings to absorb their pickup, delivery, taxi, and visitor queues off-street.
9. **Destination street-impact budgets—0.08:** Give major campuses, malls, hospitals, and venues a binding ceiling on peak vehicle entries and curb-dwell minutes.
10. **Building service-round cooperatives—0.06:** Let several buildings combine maintenance, deliveries, returns, inspections, and waste collection into shared provider rounds.
11. **Remote fleet holding—0.28:** Require ride-hail, taxi, and delivery vehicles to wait outside saturated districts until a passenger or parcel is ready.
12. **Fleet intersection reservations—0.19:** Allow predictable commercial fleets to reserve narrow junction-passage windows and route themselves around those commitments.
13. **Destination-coded platoon waves—0.05:** Release fleet vehicles in short groups sharing a downstream destination so turns and merges occur in batches.
14. **Reversible access cells—0.13:** Temporarily make saturated local cells exit-only, restoring entry after internal occupancy falls below a threshold.
15. **Guaranteed-space entry tokens—0.24:** Allow vehicles into parking-search hotspots only when they hold a confirmed garage or legal curb space.
16. **Block mobility lobbies—0.11:** Create shared off-street pickup and loading vestibules serving multiple older buildings that cannot absorb queues individually.

## CONVERGE

### Survivors

**Curb operating system:** Controlled dwell reduces double-parking and merge turbulence, which restores junction discharge capacity; it assumes enough legal curb space exists, city enforcement adopts it because violations become machine-verifiable, and one instrumented block can falsify it by showing no fall in obstruction minutes.

**Backpressure gates:** Holding vehicles upstream prevents downstream spillback, which preserves network throughput; it assumes upstream queues can be stored safely, traffic operations adopts it because rules respond automatically to occupancy, and a three-junction trial fails if total person-delay merely moves rather than falls.

**Rapid-clearance pods:** Faster obstruction removal shortens capacity-loss incidents, which prevents secondary queues; it assumes incidents materially contribute to unreliability, operators retain it because response targets are measurable, and historical replay can falsify it by showing little delay attributable to clearance time.

**Peak no-left-turn mesh:** Removing conflict phases increases junction discharge, which moves the saturation threshold upward; it assumes detours do not create worse bottlenecks, drivers comply because the geometry is unambiguous, and a weekend trial fails if network travel time rises.

**Queue-neutral building code:** Moving private queues off public lanes removes recurring blockages, which restores street capacity; it assumes sites can create or share internal space, owners comply to retain operating permits, and temporary barriers can test whether relocating one building’s queue changes corridor delay.

**Destination street-impact budgets:** Making trip generators accountable causes them to eliminate or absorb vehicle arrivals, which lowers demand at the precise bottlenecks they create; it assumes destinations can control suppliers, staff, visitors, and parking access, they maintain it because compliance preserves full access, and shadow accounting fails it if arrivals prove largely uncontrollable.

**Building service-round cooperatives:** Aggregating fragmented supplier visits removes vehicle trips, which lowers both road demand and curb dwell; it assumes buildings share enough recurring providers, managers retain it if costs and complaints fall, and three buildings can falsify it by showing negligible reduction in weekly vehicle visits.

**Remote fleet holding:** Preventing premature fleet arrivals removes circulating and idling vehicles, which cuts curb turbulence; it assumes dispatch platforms can enforce release rules, fleets retain it because failed pickups and parking tickets decline, and geofenced comparison fails it if empty vehicle-kilometres increase.

### Pairwise knockout

* **Curb operating system defeats guaranteed-space tokens:** it addresses deliveries, taxis, services, and pickups rather than parking search alone.
* **Backpressure gates defeat the no-left-turn mesh:** they respond to actual saturation and avoid imposing detours when the network is uncongested.
* **Queue-neutral building rules defeat remote fleet holding:** they assign responsibility to every queue-generating destination rather than selected fleets.
* **Service-round cooperatives defeat neighborhood caches:** they remove more trip types without requiring inventory, premises, or working capital.
* **Curb operating system defeats rapid-clearance pods:** it acts continuously on a larger share of congestion rather than only during incidents.
* **Destination budgets defeat queue-neutral building rules as a wildcard:** budgets can affect existing buildings and force operational invention beyond physical retrofits.
* **Service-round cooperatives defeat destination-coded platoons:** fewer trips require less coordination than synchronizing vehicles already on the road.
* **Curb operating system narrowly defeats backpressure gates overall:** both could matter greatly, but curb control is easier to pilot, explain, enforce, and reverse.

## Recommendations

### Best practical idea: Curb operating system

**Non-obvious insight:** A road can have adequate through-capacity yet fail because a few unpredictable curb maneuvers repeatedly consume an active lane and disrupt junction discharge.

Create a ten-block pilot in which commercial loading, ride-hail pickup, service visits, and short stops require digital bay reservations. Assign dwell limits by activity, provide legal overflow locations, and use cameras for automatic warnings before penalties.

**First cheap test:** Alternate treated and untreated blocks for four weeks; kill or redesign the idea unless lane-obstruction minutes and travel-time variability fall substantially without increasing failed pickups or illegal stopping nearby.

### Second practical idea: Backpressure gates

**Non-obvious insight:** Gridlock is a phase transition: temporarily holding a small upstream queue can prevent a much larger downstream network from losing most of its capacity.

At several critical entries, use downstream occupancy and queue sensors to block new entry whenever the receiving block lacks storage. Continue allowing exits so the saturated area drains.

**First cheap test:** Run the rule during selected peaks at three junctions; reject it if total person-delay does not fall or if unsafe queues emerge outside the controlled area.

### Best wildcard: Destination street-impact budgets

**Non-obvious insight:** A hospital, campus, mall, or venue can coordinate the traffic it generates more effectively than a city can coordinate thousands of independent drivers.

Set a measurable ceiling for vehicle entries, blocking minutes, and curb-dwell minutes around one major destination. Let the destination choose its response, but count adjacent-hour displacement and surrounding streets so it cannot export the problem.

**First cheap test:** Conduct six weeks of shadow measurement, followed by a voluntary 10% reduction target; kill the idea if the destination cannot materially influence its measured impact without degrading essential access.

### Second wildcard: Building service-round cooperatives

**Non-obvious insight:** Many urban trips exist because every apartment block and supplier optimizes separately; congestion is partly organizational duplication rather than resident demand.

Have several neighboring buildings jointly aggregate repairs, inspections, returns, routine deliveries, waste movements, and contractor visits into shared rounds or common service appointments.

**First cheap test:** Run an opt-in cooperative across three buildings for six weeks; stop if vehicle visits per occupied unit do not fall by at least 15% or if service delays and resident complaints materially increase.
