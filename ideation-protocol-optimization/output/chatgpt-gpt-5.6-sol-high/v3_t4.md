## FRAME

**Binding constraint:** Books deliver finished language faster than readers can build, test, and retain an internal model; changing the model-building bottleneck matters more than changing the medium.

**The 30-second mode—banned, including thin disguises:**

1. Audiobooks, dramatizations, or text-to-speech: changing the sensory channel.
2. Summaries, abridgments, snippets, or microlearning: reducing the payload.
3. Hypertext, branching narratives, or choose-your-own-path books: adding navigation choices.
4. Quizzes, points, streaks, puzzles, or gamification: adding extrinsic engagement.
5. AI tutors, character chats, or conversational explainers: outsourcing interpretation.
6. Book clubs, shared annotations, or collaborative reading: adding other readers.
7. Video, AR, VR, multimedia, or algorithmically personalized sequencing: adding immersion or adaptation.

No candidate below depends primarily on those mechanisms.

## DIVERGE

### Dimensions

1. **Sequence:** canonical, reverse-causal, interleaved, cyclic, or event-triggered.
2. **Information state:** complete, withheld, contradictory, redundant, or decaying.
3. **Reader operation:** predict, infer, classify, reconstruct, dispute, or preserve.
4. **Timing:** continuous, delayed, situational, recurrent, or life-stage.
5. **Authority:** author-first, evidence-first, witness-first, or reader-hypothesis-first.
6. **Persistence:** permanent text, erasable text, accumulated annotations, or re-bindable structure.

### Dimension-generated candidates

C1. **Forecast Ledger** — Before every major claim or event, the reader records what they expect; the actual text then becomes corrective evidence. Typicality: **0.45**.

C2. **Counterfactual Scar** — After each turning point, the reader records the smallest change that would reverse the outcome or conclusion. Typicality: **0.18**.

C3. **Rule-Later Edition** — Cases and examples appear first; the reader writes the governing rule before the author’s explanation is revealed. Typicality: **0.26**.

C4. **Negative-Space Edition** — Central scenes or propositions are absent, leaving only causes, consequences, and references from which they must be inferred. Typicality: **0.09**.

C5. **Eroding Pages** — Readable text gradually vanishes while the reader’s marks remain, turning the surviving book into a record of what was preserved. Typicality: **0.06**.

C6. **Memory Shadow Index** — Before seeing the real index, the reader constructs one from memory and uses the mismatch to expose their mental model. Typicality: **0.11**.

C7. **Contradiction-First Edition** — The book opens with its apparent contradictions; the main text is read as an attempt to reconcile them. Typicality: **0.17**.

C8. **Metadata-Blind Edition** — Title, author, genre, character names, and ideological labels are withheld until the end to suppress framing effects. Typicality: **0.14**.

C9. **Context-Locked Edition** — Passages are opened only after specified real-life situations occur, such as losing an argument, breaking a promise, waiting, or grieving. Typicality: **0.07**.

C10. **Life-Stage Palimpsest** — The same immutable text is reread at scheduled life milestones while every earlier annotation remains visible. Typicality: **0.21**.

C11. **Reader-Censored Edition** — Readers permanently strike anything they reject, leaving a residual book whose absences reveal their trust boundaries. Typicality: **0.10**.

C12. **Causal Rebinding** — After the first reading, pages are rebound into the causal order the reader believes underlies the author’s presentation. Typicality: **0.16**.

C13. **Model-Boundary Edition** — Chapters begin only where the reader’s governing mental model must change, rather than where the topic or chronology changes. Typicality: **0.13**.

C14. **Consequence-First Braid** — Effects appear long before causes, with each cause introduced only when several consequences can be unified by it. Typicality: **0.22**.

C15. **Obligation Edition** — Every section creates a real-world commitment whose observed outcome becomes part of the next reading session. Typicality: **0.31**.

C16. **Lens Rotation Edition** — Each section assigns a non-default interpretive role, such as insurer, child, rival, archivist, or future historian. Typicality: **0.27**.

### Borrowed domain 1: Error-correcting codes

Atomic structural rules:

1. Encode the same payload through independent transformations so one damaged channel does not destroy it.
2. Add parity constraints that reveal corruption without directly supplying the missing information.
3. Interleave dependencies so one local failure does not erase an entire conceptual unit.

C17. **Checksum Book** — Periodic statements can be coherent only when the reader’s interpretations of several earlier sections are mutually consistent. Typicality: **0.08**.

C18. **Transformed-Redundancy Edition** — Every central idea appears separately as a claim, exception, analogy, consequence, and limiting case, never as simple repetition. Typicality: **0.06**.

C19. **Interleaved-Dependency Edition** — Pieces needed to understand one concept are deliberately distributed among unrelated sections, preventing local fluency from masquerading as comprehension. Typicality: **0.12**.

### Borrowed domain 2: Forensic investigation

Atomic structural rules:

1. Examine observations before receiving the preferred theory.
2. Preserve provenance so every assertion can be traced to its source and transformations.
3. Keep competing hypotheses alive until genuinely discriminating evidence appears.

C20. **Evidence-Skeleton Edition** — Sources, observations, and exhibits are read before the author’s narrative or argument. Typicality: **0.24**.

C21. **Claim Passport Edition** — Every assertion is marked as observed, inferred, borrowed, disputed, or imagined before the prose surrounding it is read. Typicality: **0.20**.

C22. **Witness Dossier** — Independent accounts are presented without synthesis; only after the reader forms a theory does the author’s reconstruction appear. Typicality: **0.19**.

C23. **Hypothesis Holdout Edition** — Several explanations remain explicitly viable until a later passage contains evidence capable of eliminating some of them. Typicality: **0.15**.

C24. **Chain-of-Custody Margins** — Margins show how each fact travelled from event to witness to document to author, including every interpretive transformation. Typicality: **0.10**.

### Causal merging

C1 and C2 merge as **Forecast Ledger**: both use precommitted expectations to create informative prediction error.

C5 and C6 merge as **Eroding Edition**: both externalize what survives memory after access to the original weakens.

C12 and C14 merge as **Causal Rebinding**: both force reconstruction of causal order from presentation order.

C17, C18, and C19 merge as **Error-Correcting Edition**: independent encodings, checksums, and interleaving form one resilience mechanism.

C20, C21, and C24 merge as **Provenance-First Edition**: each separates evidence lineage from authorial synthesis.

C22 and C23 merge as **Forensic Dossier**: both maintain competing explanations until discriminating evidence arrives.

This leaves **16 distinct mechanisms**, four times the four eventual recommendations.

## CONVERGE

Negative-Space and Reader-Censored are removed because reader-generated omissions may strengthen misconceptions rather than correct them.

Contradiction-First and Metadata-Blind are removed because they alter framing but do not reliably produce a better internal model.

Causal Rebinding and Lens Rotation are removed because their required operations may become interpretive busywork.

Obligation and Provenance-First are removed because their causal link is strong only for narrow genres or behaviors.

### Survivors

**Forecast Ledger** — Prediction before prose creates visible error, visible error demands model revision, and model revision moves reading from passive recognition to active construction; it assumes predictions remain useful rather than tiresome; reflective readers adopt it for immediate surprise and keep it when errors become informative; falsify it by testing whether a short prototype improves next-day explanation or is mostly skipped.

**Rule-Later Edition** — Examples produce a provisional rule, the author’s rule corrects that hypothesis, and the contrast creates a constructed model rather than a copied one; it assumes examples discriminate among plausible rules; students and practical learners retain it when it improves transfer to unfamiliar cases; falsify it by comparing performance on one novel problem after a rewritten chapter.

**Eroding Edition** — Weakening access to the source forces selection and reconstruction, which makes the reader’s retained structure explicit; it assumes loss produces reflection rather than anxiety or compensatory photography; poetry, manifesto, and art-book readers retain it because each copy becomes unique; falsify it with ten simulated disappearing pages and observe whether readers reflect, circumvent, or abandon.

**Context-Locked Edition** — Matching text to lived circumstances raises relevance, relevance intensifies encoding, and stronger encoding improves integration; it assumes relevant situations occur often enough without destroying continuity; readers of philosophy, memoir, field guides, and self-development books retain it because passages arrive when usable; falsify it with eight sealed passages over ten days and compare contextual opening with immediate opening.

**Life-Stage Palimpsest** — Fixed prose plus accumulated annotations makes changes in the reader visible, turning rereading into information about the self; it assumes delayed returns justify keeping and reopening the object; gift-book, coming-of-age, and philosophical readers retain it as a longitudinal record; falsify it with three rereads across three months and measure return rates and meaningful annotation change.

**Model-Boundary Edition** — Organizing sections around conceptual breakdowns makes each required revision salient, thereby reducing unnoticed jumps in the reader’s model; it assumes authors can identify genuine model transitions; complex-nonfiction readers retain it because difficult changes become locatable; falsify it by resegmenting one dense essay and comparing readers’ causal maps.

**Error-Correcting Edition** — Independent textual encodings make misunderstandings detectable and recoverable, allowing a coherent model despite lapses or missed passages; it assumes transformations contribute independent evidence rather than decorative repetition; technical readers retain it because partial attention no longer causes silent collapse; falsify it by removing one encoding from a microbook and testing whether readers can reconstruct the idea.

**Forensic Dossier** — Evidence before synthesis forces readers to maintain and eliminate hypotheses rather than inherit the narrator’s conclusion; it assumes ambiguity creates inquiry rather than confusion; readers of history, biography, investigation, and mystery retain it because conclusions feel earned; falsify it by comparing belief revision and completion against a conventional chapter.

### Pairwise knockout

The knockout question throughout: **Which is more consequential if it works and more likely to survive contact with actual readers and publishers?**

#### Practical bracket

**Forecast Ledger vs Rule-Later:** Rule-Later wins because it creates deeper generative effort without interrupting every section.

**Model-Boundary vs Forensic Dossier:** Model-Boundary wins because it applies beyond evidence-heavy genres and requires no withholding of the author’s synthesis.

**Rule-Later vs Model-Boundary:** Rule-Later wins because its effect is directly measurable through transfer, and a publisher can prototype it by rewriting only a few pages.

#### Wildcard bracket

**Eroding vs Context-Locked:** Context-Locked wins because it changes timing without provoking loss anxiety or circumvention.

**Life-Stage Palimpsest vs Error-Correcting:** Error-Correcting wins because its benefit appears within one reading rather than requiring years of continued use.

**Context-Locked vs Error-Correcting:** Context-Locked wins because a low-cost physical prototype can test its central claim before developing a new authoring grammar.

## RECOMMENDATIONS

### Best practical: Rule-Later Edition

A chapter consists of repeated four-part units:

1. Several carefully selected cases.
2. A blank space where the reader states the rule they believe explains them.
3. The author’s rule and reasoning.
4. One adversarial case that separates the reader’s rule from the author’s.

The non-obvious insight is that **the explanation becomes more useful after the reader has generated a wrong or incomplete explanation**. The failed hypothesis creates a precise location into which the author’s model can fit.

The cheapest test is to rewrite twelve pages of an existing instructional book in this form. Randomly assign readers to the original or rewritten version, then give both groups an unfamiliar application problem. Kill the concept if transfer does not improve or abandonment rises sharply.

### Practical runner-up: Model-Boundary Edition

The table of contents names transformations rather than topics: “From objects to systems,” “From intention to incentives,” or “From correlation to mechanism.” Each new chapter begins with three elements: the old model, the anomaly it cannot explain, and the revised model required by the following pages.

The non-obvious insight is that **readers usually become lost at invisible model changes, not at difficult sentences**. Conventional chaptering by subject, length, or chronology conceals the moment when the reader must replace one explanatory structure with another.

The cheapest test is to resegment one dense essay without changing any sentences. Ask readers to draw its causal structure and identify what changed at each section boundary. Kill it if the new boundaries do not improve those maps.

### Best wildcard: Context-Locked Edition

The book contains sealed passages labelled by conditions rather than numbers:

> Open after you have defended a position you no longer believe.
> Open while waiting for someone who is late.
> Open the morning after making a promise.
> Open after discarding something you once valued.

Some passages may remain unopened for months. The reader’s life, rather than an algorithm, determines the reading order.

The non-obvious insight is that **the primary synchronization problem may not be attention but relevance**. A paragraph read at the right moment can outperform a chapter read in the correct sequence.

The cheapest test is an eight-envelope booklet distributed for ten days. Give a control group the same passages immediately. Kill the format if contextual readers neither remember nor apply the passages better, or if too many envelopes remain unopened.

### Wildcard runner-up: Error-Correcting Edition

Each central idea is encoded in four distant textual forms: a direct proposition, a counterexample, an analogy, and a consequence. Later “checksum” sentences become contradictory when any one form has been misunderstood. The reader can reconstruct a missed or weakly understood section from the remaining encodings.

The non-obvious insight is that **books are currently designed as though reading were a lossless communication channel**. Real readers skip, forget, misinterpret, and lose concentration; a book could be engineered to remain decodable under those failures.

The cheapest test is a six-page microbook containing two concepts encoded four ways. Remove one encoding for some readers and deliberately make another ambiguous. Kill the format if readers cannot recover the intended concept or detect the ambiguity better than readers of conventionally repetitive prose.
