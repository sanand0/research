## FRAME

**Binding constraint:** Meditation remains an optional destination users must remember to visit; DAU will not move materially until the app attaches immediate, credible value to an unavoidable daily moment.

**30-second mode—banned, including thin disguises:**

1. Better-timed pushes, reminders, email nudges, or “smart” notifications.
2. Streaks, badges, points, leaderboards, challenges, rewards, or loss aversion.
3. More content, celebrity teachers, daily recommendations, or generative sessions.
4. Generic personalization based on goals, mood, duration, or onboarding quizzes.
5. Friends, communities, accountability partners, group sessions, or social proof.
6. Discounts, free trials, referral programs, influencer campaigns, or broad partnerships.
7. Faster onboarding, prettier UX, reduced clicks, or other usability-only improvements.

No candidate below relies primarily on reminding, rewarding, expanding content, social pressure, discounting, acquisition, or interface polish.

## DIVERGE

### Independent dimensions

Trigger: calendar event, risky action, device behaviour, physical object, physiology, existing compulsion.
Timing: before stress, during escalation, immediately after, at a transition, during a pleasurable ritual.
Value: calm, avoided regret, restored attention, task completion, sensory enhancement, proof of efficacy.
Actor: individual, employer, operating system, browser, wearable, physical environment.
Dose: one breath, 20 seconds, two minutes, ambient silence, adaptive duration.
Control model: voluntary launch, contextual offer, interlock, automatic environmental default.

### Borrowed domain 1: Industrial safety systems

* **I1:** Detect leading indicators rather than waiting for failure.
* **I2:** Put the intervention at the exact point where the harmful action occurs.
* **I3:** Restore a safe state before allowing normal operation to resume.

### Borrowed domain 2: Tax withholding

* **T1:** Attach collection to an unavoidable transaction.
* **T2:** Make tiny default contributions instead of requiring deliberate lump-sum action.
* **T3:** Spread the burden across frequent events so each contribution feels negligible.

Candidates marked `[I]` or `[T]` require the borrowed rule; without it, their mechanism disappears.

### Causal merges

Calendar gaps, commutes, and geofence departures became **Transition Tax**; QR codes, NFC mugs, and bathroom prompts became **Physical Anchor Kit**; message delays, purchase pauses, and post-abuse recovery became **Risk-Event Interlock**; app redirects and URL substitutions became **Compulsion Tollbooth**.

### Post-merge candidate pool

1. **Transition Tax `[T1,T3]`** — Attach a 45–90-second reset to the end of dense calendar events so existing transitions become meditation transactions; typicality **0.34**.
2. **Compulsion Tollbooth `[T1,T2]`** — Redirect the first daily launch of a user-selected compulsive app through a 30-second grounding action; typicality **0.12**.
3. **Risk-Event Interlock `[I2,I3]`** — Insert one deliberate breath before an emotionally charged message, impulse purchase, trade, or re-entry after an abusive call; typicality **0.07**.
4. **Fragmentation Circuit Breaker `[I1]`** — Detect local signals such as tab thrashing or rapid app switching and offer a 20-second attentional reset; typicality **0.10**.
5. **Physical Anchor Kit** — Let users place NFC or QR anchors beside an unavoidable daily object such as a toothbrush, kettle, or work badge; typicality **0.20**.
6. **Personal Efficacy Ledger** — Record one-tap state before and after sessions, then show context-specific evidence such as “presentation resets usually reduce tension by two levels”; typicality **0.29**.
7. **Calm-to-Action Bridge** — End each session by converting the calmer state into one concrete action that closes the stress-producing loop; typicality **0.23**.
8. **Silent Dose `[I3]`** — Replace guided content with a haptic-started period of DND, screen-off silence, and a haptic finish; typicality **0.17**.
9. **Alarm Interlock `[T1,T2]`** — Make three paced breaths an optional alternative gesture for dismissing the first morning alarm; typicality **0.24**.
10. **Future-Self Inoculation** — Before a recurring difficult event, replay a 15-second message the user recorded while calm for their future stressed self; typicality **0.18**.
11. **Physiological Microdose `[I1]`** — Use on-device wearable signals to offer the minimum effective dose when arousal crosses a personal threshold; typicality **0.27**.
12. **Pleasure Primer** — Place 20 seconds of silence before coffee, music, gaming, or a meal, positioning meditation as sensory amplification rather than stress treatment; typicality **0.09**.
13. **Face-Down Trigger `[T1]`** — Starting when the phone is deliberately placed face down, deliver a haptic breathing rhythm without requiring navigation; typicality **0.06**.
14. **Recurrence Matcher** — Recognize a previously logged stress pattern and retrieve the short intervention that measurably helped last time; typicality **0.19**.

Eleven of fourteen candidates score below 0.3 typicality.

## CONVERGE

### Survivors

* **Transition Tax:** Calendar transition → contextual micro-session → repeated immediate recovery → DAU; assumes enough users have frequent scheduled transitions; calendar users adopt it to protect focus and retain it if post-meeting strain falls; falsify by attaching reset links to 200 real events and observing whether fewer than 10% launch twice in one week.
* **Compulsion Tollbooth:** Existing compulsive launch → unavoidable grounding step → daily piggybacked use → DAU; assumes users tolerate limited friction; heavy social-media users adopt it for self-control and retain it if they do not disable the rule; falsify with an Android or iOS Shortcuts prototype and measure seven-day bypass or deletion.
* **Risk-Event Interlock:** High-consequence moment → brief state reset → avoided regret or error → memorable value → repeated use; assumes the app can identify or be invoked at the right moment; impulsive communicators adopt it to prevent damage and retain it after one visibly avoided mistake; falsify with a Gmail/Slack browser extension and compare send-cancellation or rewrite rates.
* **Fragmentation Circuit Breaker:** Behavioural leading signal → reset before exhaustion → restored attention → repeat use; assumes tab or app switching predicts subjective fragmentation; knowledge workers adopt it for focus and retain it if false interventions remain rare; falsify by comparing detected events with one-tap “yes, fragmented” labels.
* **Physical Anchor Kit:** Unavoidable object interaction → external cue → session without remembering → DAU; assumes users will install an anchor in a stable location; routine-driven users adopt it because it removes memory and retain it if tapping becomes automatic; falsify with printable QR cards and seven-day scan recurrence.
* **Personal Efficacy Ledger:** Before/after evidence → belief that meditation works in specific contexts → intentional return → DAU; assumes one-tap self-report is credible and not burdensome; sceptical users adopt it for proof and retain it when patterns become visible; falsify by testing whether displaying personal effect summaries increases next-week sessions over collecting ratings silently.
* **Calm-to-Action Bridge:** Meditation → reduced avoidance → immediate completion of the stress-producing task → practical utility → repeat use; assumes calm reliably improves action initiation; procrastinators adopt it to get unstuck and retain it if they complete the selected action; falsify by measuring whether users perform the chosen action within ten minutes.
* **Silent Dose:** Remove content choice and stimulation → lower activation energy → more completed sessions → DAU; assumes content fatigue is a material barrier; experienced or overstimulated users adopt it for simplicity and retain it if silent sessions repeat; falsify by offering silent mode beside normal sessions and comparing repeat completion.

### Pairwise knockout

**Transition Tax defeats Physical Anchor Kit:** Both externalize the cue, but calendar attachment scales without physical installation and carries richer context.

**Compulsion Tollbooth defeats Silent Dose:** Silent Dose still requires initiation; Tollbooth borrows an already dominant daily behaviour.

**Risk-Event Interlock defeats Fragmentation Circuit Breaker:** Avoiding an irreversible mistake provides clearer value than correcting an inferred attention state, with fewer ambiguous triggers.

**Personal Efficacy Ledger defeats Calm-to-Action Bridge:** The ledger can validate many meditation contexts, while the action bridge depends on stress being caused by an immediately actionable task.

**Transition Tax defeats Compulsion Tollbooth:** Tollbooth may be more frequent, but operating-system restrictions, irritation, and easy bypass threaten survival in real use.

**Risk-Event Interlock defeats Personal Efficacy Ledger:** Avoided regret is immediate and behaviourally concrete; the ledger relies on repeated self-report before its value appears.

**Transition Tax defeats Risk-Event Interlock overall:** The interlock may be more consequential per event, but Transition Tax has a wider addressable population, simpler permissions, clearer user control, and a cheaper route to reliable deployment.

## RECOMMENDATIONS

### Best practical idea: Transition Tax

**Non-obvious insight:** The scarce resource is not meditation content or motivation; it is a legitimate place in the day. Calendars already authorize short periods of protected time, especially between cognitively demanding events.

The app should identify dense or high-load meetings and append a prominent **“90-second reset”** action to the event’s end state. Users control which meeting types qualify. The session should be fixed and immediate—no browsing, mood selection, or content choice.

**First cheap test:** Recruit 100 existing users, attach reset links to eligible calendar events for two weeks, and compare against their previous two weeks. Kill the idea if fewer than 15% use it on three distinct days or if repeated launch rates are no better than generic reminders.

### Practical learning layer: Personal Efficacy Ledger

**Non-obvious insight:** Many users fail to return because meditation’s benefit is diffuse and easy to misremember, not because the benefit is absent. Personal evidence can turn an abstract wellness claim into a reliable tool-selection rule.

Add a single tap before and after selected sessions, automatically retaining the context: “after meeting,” “before sleep,” or “while angry.” Show conclusions only after enough observations exist.

**First cheap test:** Randomize users who already complete sessions into visible versus hidden summaries. Kill it if visible personal evidence does not increase distinct active days over the following fortnight.

### Best wildcard: Risk-Event Interlock

**Non-obvious insight:** Calm is worth disproportionately more immediately before an irreversible action than during a generic free moment. One prevented message, purchase, or workplace error may establish stronger retention than dozens of pleasant sessions.

Begin with emotionally charged email and chat: detect rapid rewriting, high typing velocity, or manually marked conversations locally, then offer a single-breath delay before sending. Do not make meditation mandatory.

**First cheap test:** Build a browser extension for Gmail or Slack with a user-invoked “pause before send” button before attempting automatic detection. Kill it if fewer than 20% of users invoke it twice or if fewer than 10% subsequently revise, delay, or cancel the message.

### Second wildcard: Compulsion Tollbooth

**Non-obvious insight:** A meditation habit does not need to manufacture its own cue; it can parasitize a stronger habit whose users already want to weaken.

Let users choose one high-frequency app. On its first launch after a configurable usage threshold, route them through a 30-second reset, with a visible bypass and a strict once-per-day maximum.

**First cheap test:** Prototype with Android accessibility controls and iOS Shortcuts rather than building OS-level infrastructure. Kill it if more than one-third of activated users bypass, disable, or uninstall the mechanism during the first week.
