## 1. BAN: the obvious mode

The following ideas—and thin disguises of them—are excluded:

1. Congestion pricing, tolls, tradable road credits, or paid permits.
2. More public transport capacity, bus lanes, or rail expansion.
3. Cycling and walking infrastructure or mode-shift campaigns.
4. Carpooling, HOV lanes, ride-sharing, or higher vehicle occupancy.
5. Remote work, virtual appointments, staggered hours, or off-peak incentives.
6. Parking reduction, car-free zones, odd–even rules, or vehicle-access restrictions.
7. Road widening, flyovers, adaptive signals, navigation, or traffic rerouting.

The ideas below instead alter tasks, destinations, institutions, logistics, entitlements, and disruption recovery.

## Borrowed domain 1: distributed computer systems

Three atomic structural rules:

1. **Edge caching:** replicate frequently requested functions near users rather than repeatedly transporting requests to a central server.
2. **Batching:** combine many small operations so that setup and transport costs are paid once.
3. **Deduplication:** detect overlapping requests and execute their common components only once.

Candidates that structurally depend on these rules:

* **Civic Edge Cache:** physically replicate frequently visited services across neighbourhoods.
* **Building Logistics Kernel:** treat hundreds of tenants’ deliveries and service requests as one aggregated workload.
* **Household Trip-Purpose Compiler:** combine overlapping errands across households before assigning someone to perform them.
* **Parcel Address Rewriter:** redirect deliveries to the neighbourhood handoff point that minimizes duplicated courier visits.
* **Anchor Piggyback Hubs:** attach multiple services to a destination people already have to visit.

A “smart-city monitoring dashboard” was dropped: it borrows computing language but none of the causal structure.

## Borrowed domain 2: kidney-exchange systems

Three atomic structural rules:

1. **Cycles unlock matches:** A cannot swap directly with B, but A→B→C→A may satisfy everyone.
2. **Preserve entitlement, change counterpart:** participants keep the value they were promised while receiving it from a different provider or location.
3. **Make compatibility explicit:** matching works only when quality, timing, qualifications, and constraints are machine-checkable.

Candidates that depend on these rules:

* **Vendor Job Exchange:** service firms exchange geographically inconvenient jobs through multi-party cycles.
* **School-Seat Swap Cycles:** families exchange assigned places while preserving capacity and programme constraints.
* **Clinic-Slot Portability Exchange:** patients receive an equivalent specialist slot nearer home.
* **Housing–Job Swap Chains:** households exchange equivalent residences to reduce aggregate commute distance.

A “donor-style congestion awareness campaign” was dropped because the borrowing was merely rhetorical.

## Survivor critiques

| Survivor                                 | Assumption that must hold                                                                                      | Cheapest falsifying observation                                                                                                       | Why obvious ideas miss it                                                                                           |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Service Edge Network**                 | A substantial share of journeys repeatedly target services that can be replicated or fulfilled by partners     | In one district, classify two weeks of trips by destination and test whether the top replaceable services represent meaningful volume | Conventional approaches transport people more efficiently instead of relocating the service                         |
| **Household Task Compiler**              | Households will delegate sufficiently standardized errands                                                     | Run a manual concierge for two apartment blocks and measure accepted tasks and net journeys removed                                   | Trip planning normally optimizes each household separately rather than combining demand across households           |
| **Building Logistics Kernel**            | Many building-bound vehicle visits can be consolidated without unacceptable service loss                       | Give one large building a shared receiving and service dispatcher; compare vehicle entries with a similar control building            | Traffic policy treats residents and firms as individual actors, overlooking the building as an aggregation boundary |
| **Vendor Job Exchange**                  | Competing firms can specify equivalent work and settle exchanges without exposing sensitive data               | Offline-match 100 completed jobs from three firms and calculate distance savings and incompatibility rates                            | Dispatch systems optimize within company boundaries even when the citywide optimum requires crossing them           |
| **Entitlement-Preserving Seat Exchange** | Many users value proximity but fear losing quality, eligibility or institutional rights                        | Run an anonymized match on existing school or clinic records without implementing any swaps                                           | Normal reassignment proposals ask people to surrender entitlements; exchange cycles need not                        |
| **Trip-Generator Reliability Regime**    | A manageable number of institutions cause a disproportionate share of recurrent peaks and can alter operations | Rank the largest destinations around several bottlenecks and test whether their activity predicts congestion recurrence               | Conventional policy governs travellers and roads, not the organizations generating synchronized journeys            |
| **Trip-Deletion Procurement**            | Removed trips can be measured and attributed without easy gaming                                               | Offer a small contract for one auditable recurring journey category and compare independent counts                                    | Transport procurement normally purchases infrastructure or service, not non-occurrence                              |
| **Anchor Piggyback Hubs**                | People have unavoidable anchor journeys with spare dwell time and unmet adjacent needs                         | Place temporary service booths at three schools or workplaces and count separate journeys displaced                                   | Conventional co-location starts with real estate; this starts with journeys that already exist                      |
| **Event Mirroring Network**              | Enough of an event’s value survives when participation is distributed                                          | Run one satellite venue for a festival, lecture or civic event and measure substitution from the central site                         | Event management optimizes attendance and revenue, rarely the topology of arrival demand                            |

| **Capacity-Outage Compression**          | Incidents and fragmented street works account for a material share of delay, and their duration is reducible   | Examine the fifty worst congestion episodes and estimate the removable minutes in each                                                | Most congestion strategies target steady-state demand and neglect temporary capacity failures                       |

## Pairwise knockout: “most likely to matter for this brief”

### Opening round

* **Service Edge Network defeats Event Mirroring.** It attacks everyday repeat journeys rather than occasional event spikes.
* **Anchor Piggyback Hubs defeat Household Task Compiler.** They require less household trust and behavioural change.
* **Building Logistics Kernel defeats Capacity-Outage Compression.** It applies every day across many streets, rather than only during disruptions.
* **Vendor Job Exchange defeats Housing–Job Swap Chains.** Jobs are easier to reassign than homes.
* **Entitlement-Preserving Seat Exchange defeats Trip-Deletion Procurement.** It has a concrete trip-removal mechanism and less measurement gaming.
* **Trip-Generator Reliability Regime receives the bye.** It is the only survivor that can compel action across several mechanisms and institutions.

### Second round

* **Service Edge Network defeats Anchor Piggyback Hubs.** Permanent local capacity covers more trip purposes than a limited set of existing anchors.
* **Building Logistics Kernel defeats Vendor Job Exchange.** A city can pilot it without first creating trust and settlement rules among competitors.
* **Trip-Generator Reliability Regime defeats Entitlement-Preserving Seat Exchange.** It can address schools, hospitals, offices, malls and venues rather than one allocation system.

### Practical final

* **Building Logistics Kernel defeats Service Edge Network.** It needs fewer new facilities and can begin with existing buildings and operators.
* **Trip-Generator Reliability Regime defeats Building Logistics Kernel.** It has greater leverage: it can make the highest-impact institutions adopt building aggregation, local fulfilment, task consolidation or other non-banned remedies.

### Wildcard knockout

* **Vendor Job Exchange defeats Housing–Job Swap Chains.** It preserves homes and employment relationships while moving only the assigned task.
* **Entitlement-Preserving Seat Exchange defeats Event Mirroring.** It applies to recurring daily journeys and not just exceptional gatherings.
* **Entitlement-Preserving Seat Exchange defeats Vendor Job Exchange.** School and healthcare journeys are frequent, highly patterned and tied to rights that the exchange mechanism uniquely preserves.

# Recommendations

## Best practical idea: Trip-Generator Reliability Regime

**Non-obvious insight:** Congestion is not produced solely by independent drivers. Large institutions behave like synchronized traffic generators. Cities regulate road users and road capacity, but seldom require a hospital, school, mall, office campus or venue to meet a measurable congestion-reliability obligation.

Each major generator would receive a small set of outcome measures, such as recurrent vehicle arrivals attributable to the site, queue spillback episodes, or network delay around its operating footprint. Repeated failures would trigger a factual postmortem and an institution-specific corrective plan. The city specifies outcomes, not a banned remedy.

**First cheap test:** Using four weeks of traffic counts and destination activity, rank twenty large institutions near two recurrent bottlenecks. Test whether a few institutions’ operating patterns reliably predict the congestion episodes. Invite the top three to trial operational changes and compare against matched untreated sites.

## Second practical idea: Building Logistics Kernel

**Non-obvious insight:** A 500-unit building currently behaves like 500 unrelated logistics customers. Every resident, tenant, courier and maintenance vendor independently incurs vehicle setup, access and handling costs. The building is a natural transaction-compression layer.

A building kernel could combine receiving, returns, waste movement, routine maintenance, food supply and service calls; use shared custody records; and assign fulfilment to consolidated carriers or crews.

**First cheap test:** Recruit one large residential or mixed-use building. Add a common receiving point and a human dispatcher who can consolidate selected deliveries and service jobs. Measure vehicle entries, failed deliveries, completion time and resident satisfaction against a comparable building for six weeks.

## Best wildcard: Entitlement-Preserving Seat Exchange

**Non-obvious insight:** People often travel far not because they insist on a particular building, but because their entitlement—school programme, specialist qualification, insurance coverage, seniority or service quality—is attached to that building. Exchange cycles can change locations without asking participants to surrender the entitlement they value.

A direct swap may be impossible, while a four- or five-party cycle can satisfy capacity, programme, clinical and distance constraints simultaneously.

**First cheap test:** Take anonymized records from one school system or clinic network. Compute distance-reducing exchanges while holding capacity, eligibility and quality constraints fixed. Before moving anyone, show hypothetical matches to affected users. Kill the idea if feasible cycles are rare or users reject most technically valid exchanges.
