## FRAME

**Binding constraint:** Patients' intent to attend is captured once, at booking — often weeks in advance — and nothing in the system tracks, cheapens, or reconfirms that commitment as life intervenes between then and the appointment. No-shows are the visible symptom of an *uncorrected commitment decay* over time, not a lack of reminders.

**Mode (banned, plus thin disguises of each):**
1. SMS/text reminders (and any reminder-timing tweak)
2. Phone call reminders
3. Overbooking to compensate for expected no-shows
4. No-show fees / financial penalties on the patient
5. Shortening lead time slightly
6. App-based "confirm your appointment" buttons
7. Generic email reminders with a reschedule link

No later candidate may just be a reminder, a fee, or overbooking wearing a costume.

## DIVERGE

**Dimensions:** mechanism (info/incentive/social/physical/architecture) · actor (patient/staff/peer/algorithm/third party) · who pays · timing (booking/mid-wait/hours-before/at-slot/after) · what's removed or inverted (the trip, the fixed slot, the far-advance booking, single ownership of the ticket).

Candidates (typicality score = odds a generic AI assistant offers this):
- Real-time standby pool that instantly re-fills any released slot — 0.15
- Rolling claim window: patients grab slots within 72h instead of booking weeks out — 0.2
- Patient-to-patient appointment resale/trade board — 0.1
- Community health worker home visit 48h before, troubleshooting barriers in person — 0.35
- Peer accountability network: nominated buddy also gets notified, optional loss-framed deposit held by a peer, not the hospital — 0.15
- Group/batch scheduling of patients with similar conditions (co-presence effect) — 0.25
- Open-access/same-day scheduling model (redesign capacity itself) — 0.3
- Auto-arranged, hospital-paid transport bundled into the booking — 0.3
- Benefit-escrow: attendance unlocks another resource the patient already wants (refill, voucher) — 0.1
- Satellite mini-clinics placed inside sites patients already visit weekly (church, workplace, grocery store) — 0.3
- Social-norm messaging ("87% of patients like you kept this visit") — 0.5

7 of 11 score below 0.3. ✅

**Borrowed structural rules:**
- *Group fitness classes*: attendance is socially visible; cohorts build relational stakes over repeated meetings → feeds group/batch scheduling and peer accountability.
- *Airline/ride-hailing matching*: overbooking absorbs no-shows as given rather than preventing them; tickets are transferable; matching happens near the moment of need → feeds standby pool, resale board, rolling claim window.

**Merge:** standby pool + rolling claim window → one mechanism ("real-time slot absorption/dynamic claiming"). Peer buddy + loss-framed deposit stay merged as "peer accountability network" (same social-capital mechanism, incentive variant folded in).

Surviving pool (9, spread across mechanism/actor/timing): dynamic slot absorption, resale board, community health worker visit, peer accountability network, group/batch scheduling, open-access scheduling, transit-bundling, benefit-escrow, satellite mini-clinics.

## CONVERGE

For each: assumption → who sustains it → cheap falsification test.

| Candidate | Causal path | Must-hold assumption | Falsification test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open-access/same-day scheduling | Shrinks the multi-week decay window itself → less time for forgetting/life changes to erode intent | Enough near-term capacity exists; works for routine, not scarce specialist, slots | Convert 20% of one primary-care clinic's slots to open-access; compare no-show rate to rest of clinic over 8 weeks |
| Dynamic slot absorption/claiming | Patients claim slots close to need + released slots refill instantly → decay window shrinks *and* wasted slots drop | Patients will book on short notice; needs live matching infra | Pilot on one routine follow-up list for 4 weeks, track claim-fill rate |
| Resale/trade board | Patient-to-patient handoff turns a silent no-show into an active transfer → slot utilization rises (reframes success metric) | Enough peer demand exists; clinically safe to swap patients into a slot type | Run board in one department; measure % of released slots refilled in 24h |
| Community health worker visit | In-person contact surfaces and resolves concrete barriers reminders can't reach | Barriers are logistical/informational and solvable in one conversation | Pilot in one high-no-show panel vs matched control, 2 months |
| Peer accountability network | Social visibility of intent raises the felt cost of no-showing | Patients have and will nominate a willing peer | Opt-in offer at booking; compare show-rate of opt-ins vs eligible non-participants |
| Group/batch scheduling | Co-presence + light social contract raises attendance | Patients with similar conditions can be clustered without stigma | Run for one chronic-disease clinic list 6 weeks vs matched individual scheduling |
| Transit-bundling | Removes the single biggest physical blocker for transport-insecure patients | Transport, not motivation, is the dominant blocker for that segment | Offer to flagged transport-insecure cohort; compare to similar non-offered cohort, 1 month |
| Benefit-escrow | Attendance becomes instrumental to something already wanted | Patient has another recurring desired benefit through the hospital; must stay voluntary, not coercive | Voluntary opt-in bundling pilot with one benefit-linked group |
| Satellite mini-clinics | Folds the visit into an existing routine, killing the "special trip" cost entirely | Host sites will partner; care can be safely delivered off-site | Run one mini-clinic for basic screening 6–8 weeks vs standard booking |

**Pairwise knockouts:**
- Open-access scheduling beats dynamic slot absorption: same root fix (shrink the decay window) but far less infrastructure risk, faster to pilot, no dependence on patients adopting new claiming behavior.
- Open-access scheduling beats satellite mini-clinics: mini-clinics are more consequential if fully realized but much slower and partner-dependent; open-access is nearly as consequential and testable in weeks.
- Open-access scheduling beats community health worker visits: the latter is well-evidenced but narrow (high-risk subgroup only) and expensive per patient; open-access attacks the bottleneck hospital-wide.
- Among the low-typicality survivors, resale board beats benefit-escrow (escrow carries real coercion risk and needs a pre-existing benefit tie-in) and beats dynamic absorption on simplicity (no matching algorithm needed, just a listing board) while still directly converting wasted slots into utilized ones.

## Recommendation

**Best practical idea — Open-access/same-day scheduling redesign.** Non-obvious insight: the bottleneck isn't attention at appointment time, it's that the *booking itself* is made too far from the moment of need, giving weeks for intent to decay — so the fix is structural (shrink the gap), not communicative (remind harder). First cheap test: convert 20% of one clinic's slots to next-day/same-day booking and compare no-show rate to the rest of the clinic over 8 weeks.

**Best wildcard — Patient-to-patient appointment resale/trade board.** Non-obvious insight: a no-show is only a problem because the slot goes empty, not because a specific named patient skipped it — so let patients who know in advance they can't come hand their slot to another patient who needs it, turning a silent loss into an active transfer. First cheap test: stand up a simple listing board in one department for released slots and measure what fraction get refilled within 24 hours.
