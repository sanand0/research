## CONVERGE

| Idea | Load-bearing assumption | Cheapest falsifier | Why the obvious list missed it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Colony Anchor Network | Retirees want group-level belonging more than individual flexibility | Ask nomad-retiree communities if they'd join a fixed rotating group of ~10 strangers | Defaults assume individual membership (coworking logic) |
| Symbiont Host Matching | Local businesses will accept an *ongoing* binding exchange, not a one-off | Pitch 5 local hosts in nomad hubs on a binding reciprocal contract | Housing is always modeled as a transaction, not an obligation |
| Bleach Event Protocol | A measurable stress threshold can predict crisis before it hits | Check whether real expat exits were predictable or sudden, via case studies | Insurance logic is reactive, never preventive-by-design |
| Retiree Squawk ID | Users will trade privacy (continuous broadcast) for safety | Ask target users directly if they'd wear an always-on locator | Autonomy-seeking demographic — designers assume they'd refuse, so nobody proposes it seriously |
| Sector Handoff Care Chain | Local entities will accept *liability* for a stranger's care handoff | Interview local coordinators on willingness to sign liability agreements | Requires cross-border legal infrastructure consumer apps avoid |
| Body Double Estate Trustee | Mobile retirees have a real unmet gap in POA/estate document access | Ask 5 estate lawyers if existing tools already solve this | Product design avoids mortality-salient framing |
| Pension Geo-Router | Pension payouts can legally disburse dynamically across jurisdictions | Ask 2–3 pension providers about cross-border disbursement legality | Assumed a currency problem; it's actually a tax-residency/regulatory problem |
| Snowbird Fleet Cooperative | Retiree nomads cluster on few enough predictable routes to pencil out | Check existing RV-park/Sunbelt migration data for route clustering | "Nomad" evokes software, not the physical seasonal-migration reality |
| Separation Minima Pacing Rule | Fast location-switching causally drives health crises in older travelers | Search cruise-line/expat-insurer claims data for pace-health correlation | Paternalistic constraint violates consumer-software norms, so nobody proposes overriding user choice |

**Pairwise knockout** (relevance to *this* brief: real unmet need + buildability + fit with a freedom-seeking population):
- Colony Anchor Network > Symbiont Host Matching (lower legal friction, bigger felt need)
- Retiree Squawk ID > Bleach Event Protocol (more tangible MVP)
- Body Double Estate Trustee > Sector Handoff Care Chain (no liability infrastructure needed to start)
- Pension Geo-Router > Snowbird Fleet Cooperative (bigger addressable pain point)
- Round 2: Squawk ID edges Colony Anchor on buildability, but Colony Anchor survives as wildcard on emotional need; Estate Trustee beats Geo-Router (Geo-Router's regulatory assumption is the shakiest of the whole pool)
- Final: **Estate Trustee vs. Colony Anchor** — Estate Trustee wins on "practical," Colony Anchor wins on "wildcard" (Separation Minima loses here because it strips agency from an autonomy-selected population — likely product-market rejection despite decent evidence support)

## Recommendation

**Best practical idea — Body Double Estate Trustee.** A legal-custody institution (durable POA + document vault + local-notary network + succession triggers) built specifically for people with no fixed domicile. Non-obvious insight: existing estate-planning tools assume a home jurisdiction; statelessness-by-choice is the actual gap, not death-avoidance psychology. First cheap test: interview five cross-border estate/immigration lawyers on whether "no fixed domicile POA + emergency document access" is already solved — if they all shrug and say "we just tell people to pick a state," the gap is real and buildable.

**Best wildcard — Colony Anchor Network.** A persistent, legally-registered micro-colony (8–12 people) that individuals rotate through — membership belongs to the group, not the location — borrowed directly from the coral-colony structural rule (the colony outlives the polyp). Non-obvious insight: this population didn't lose their *location*, they lost their *standing institutions* (workplace, church, block); a coworking desk doesn't replace that, but a persistent group with continuity might. First cheap test: post the concept in 2–3 existing digital-nomad-retiree online communities and measure how many would commit real money/time to joining a group they didn't personally hand-pick — if the answer is near-zero, the independence assumption kills it fast and cheaply.
