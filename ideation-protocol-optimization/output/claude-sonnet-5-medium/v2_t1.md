# BAN — the 30-second mode

A typical generalist (or AI) asked "increase DAU for meditation app" would offer:

1. **Streaks/badges/levels** (gamification) — explicitly excluded already
2. **Smarter push notifications** ("time to meditate!" personalized by ML) — explicitly excluded already
3. **Social features** — friend leaderboards, share your streak
4. **Content expansion** — more meditations, celebrity voices, sleep stories
5. **Personalization/AI coach** — recommends sessions based on mood
6. **Habit-stacking reminders** — "meditate after you brush your teeth"
7. **Referral/invite programs** — give a week free, get a week free

**Banned mechanism families:** (a) reward-for-return mechanics, (b) reminder/notification triggers, (c) social comparison/sharing, (d) content-volume increases, (e) recommendation personalization, (f) friend-referral loops. Nothing below may reduce to "notify them," "reward them," "show them to friends," or "give them more content."

# DIVERGE

### Dimension framing

1. **Mechanism** — what causes the return visit (biological, social, financial, environmental, narrative, sensory)
2. **Actor** — who initiates the session (user, app, another person, an object, an algorithm watching something else)
3. **Who pays / what's at stake** — money, social capital, data, physical object, time-locked commitment
4. **Timing trigger** — clock-based, event-based, state-based (biometric), ambient/environmental, other-app-based
5. **What's removed or inverted** — remove the app's initiative, remove the user's choice, remove privacy, remove the streak's *positive* framing (loss instead of gain), remove the solo nature of practice
6. **Session unit** — what counts as "use" (full session vs. micro-touch vs. passive vs. co-presence)

### Candidates spanning dimension-space (short titles + typicality score)

1. **Anti-Streak / Streak Insurance Market** — instead of rewarding continuation, let users "bet" real (small) money on their own future adherence, forfeited to a charity or pool if missed. Loss-framing inversion. *0.35*
2. **Ambient Co-Meditation Windows** — app surfaces a live (anonymized presence-count, not chat) "X people meditating right now near you / in your building" — social presence without social comparison. *0.3*
3. **Environmental Trigger Object** — a $2 physical token (sticker, smart-tag, or literally a printed card) placed on a mirror/door that, when picked up/touched (NFC) or just seen, is the trigger — moving initiative outside the phone entirely. *0.15*
4. **Meditation-as-Toll** — partner with apps the user *already* compulsively opens (e.g., a specific game, or doom-scroll app via Screen Time API) so a short meditation is the literal unlock cost to open them, borrowed from "friction economics." *0.1*
5. **Reverse Coaching (User Teaches)** — user records a 60-second guided clip for a stranger/new user; teaching-to-learn effect drives return more than consuming content. *0.2*
6. **State-Triggered, Not Clock-Triggered** — use phone sensors (accelerometer/HR from wearable) to detect a stress spike or a "still moment" (train delay, waiting room via calendar+location) and offer session only then. *0.25*
7. **Meditation Exchange Market** — sessions have real scarcity: user "earns" a session credit only by giving something (a kind message to a stranger user, a minute of silence recorded, a small real-world act) — economy-of-practice borrowed from mutual-aid systems. *0.15*
8. **Co-located Silent Room** — app coordinates literal shared physical silence: two friends/strangers open app simultaneously, screens show a shared subtle visual (not chat) proving synchronous presence — accountability via witnessed absence. *0.2*
9. **Debt/Grief Framing: "Skipped Session Letter"** — missed days generate a short reflective letter *from your past self* (not guilt-shaming, but narrative continuity) delivered the next time you open, borrowed from epistolary fiction structure. *0.1*
10. **Un-App: SMS/Voice-only Mode** — meditation delivered via a phone call or SMS thread, no app open required — removes the "open the app" unit of DAU entirely, forces redefinition of engagement. *0.05*
11. **Sponsor-a-Session (Someone Else Pays)** — employer/insurer/family member pre-pays and is notified (aggregate, private) of adherence — third-party stake without surveillance of content. *0.2*
12. **Meditation Passport / Physical World Unlock** — sessions tied to real-world locations (park bench, specific transit stop) using geofencing — practice becomes a scavenger-hunt-like traversal, borrowed from geocaching structural rules (physical scarcity, discovery, location-locked reward). *0.15*

### Borrowed-domain exercise

**Domain A: Farming / crop rotation**
- Atomic rules: (1) yield depends on *rest* periods, not constant input; (2) a field's soil quality is shared infrastructure across seasons — degrade it and future yield drops; (3) planting is timed to external season signals, not the farmer's mood.

**Domain B: Casino slot machine economics (the actual mechanism, not "gamification" as a label)**
- Atomic rules: (1) variable-ratio reinforcement schedules outperform fixed schedules for compulsive return; (2) the "near miss" (almost-win) drives replay more than the win itself; (3) the machine never tells you when the next win is coming — unpredictability is load-bearing.

**Candidates that require the borrowed rule (not decorative):**

13. **Fallow Weeks (Farming rule 1)** — the app *actively locks users out* one week per month ("your practice is resting"), reopening with a different modality. This only works because rest-improves-yield is real agronomy, not a gimmick — the mechanism is designed scarcity increasing perceived and actual value of return, structurally identical to crop rotation, not to "streak breaks." *0.05*
14. **Shared Soil Score (Farming rule 2)** — a *neighborhood/cohort* practice-quality metric that degrades for everyone if usage drops app-wide, visible only in aggregate (not individual shaming) — commons-management dynamics driving return out of stewardship, not personal reward. *0.1*
15. **Variable Session Reveal (Slot rule 1+3)** — user doesn't pick session content; the app conceals what today's practice will be until the moment of starting, and length is not shown until session ends — unpredictability structurally borrowed from slot design, applied to *content* not reward, to build curiosity-driven (not anxiety-driven) return. *0.1*
16. **Near-Miss Progress Bar (Slot rule 2)** — a *practice depth meter* that occasionally shows the user was "almost" at a deeper state (via HR variability data) right as they stop — genuine biometric near-miss, not manufactured, prompting "one more try" the way a near-win does. *0.05* — flag: this borders on manipulative, discuss in critique.

# CONVERGE

### Critique each survivor

**A. Anti-Streak Insurance**
- Must hold: users will pre-commit small real money against future adherence (loss aversion beats gain-seeking for this audience).
- Cheap falsifier: a landing-page/waitlist A/B test offering "put $2 at stake" vs. "earn $2 back" — if opt-in rate for the stake version is near zero, kill it.
- Missed by generalists because: they default to reward framing (streak = gain), never consider that meditation users, who are often stress-avoidant, may respond more to loss-of-small-stake than a gamified reward — but this needs validating, not assuming.

**B. Ambient Co-Presence**
- Must hold: knowing strangers/coworkers are *currently* meditating creates pull without needing content or comparison.
- Cheap falsifier: fake-door test — show a "12 people meditating near you right now" banner (no real backend) and measure session-start lift vs. control.
- Missed because: generalists jump straight to "social = leaderboard/friends," not realizing passive co-presence (like a quiet library) is a different, lower-friction social mechanism.

**C. Environmental Trigger Object**
- Must hold: a physical object outperforms a phone notification at prompting action because it's not competing with other app notifications for attention.
- Cheap falsifier: mail 200 users a physical sticker/card with instructions, measure DAU delta vs. matched cohort with only push notifications.
- Missed because: it's off-screen, and app teams default to solving app problems with app tools.

**D. Frictionless Delivery (SMS/voice)**
- Must hold: some meaningful user segment wants meditation but the *act of opening an app* is itself the drop-off point (not the content or motivation).
- Cheap falsifier: check existing funnel data — do users who set an intention (e.g., calendar reminder) but never open the app exist in large numbers? Survey lapsed users: "did you want to but not open the app?"
- Missed because: DAU is literally defined by app opens, so this idea is nearly definitionally excluded from an "increase DAU" brief — yet if the goal is really *practice frequency*, this could matter more than DAU itself, which is worth flagging as a possible brief-mismatch.

**E. State-Triggered Sessions**
- Must hold: sensor/calendar-detected "good moments" (stress spike, waiting time) convert better than clock-based reminders.
- Cheap falsifier: compare open-rates of state-triggered vs. time-triggered notifications in an A/B (this reintroduces notifications as *delivery*, but the trigger logic, not the notification itself, is the novel mechanism).
- Missed because: generalists conflate "smarter notifications" (personalization of message/time-of-day) with "different trigger source" (biometric/context vs. calendar) — the latter is structurally different.

**F. Meditation Exchange Market**
- Must hold: users are motivated by *earning access* through a small prosocial act, not just consuming freely.
- Cheap falsifier: prototype a "send a 10-second kindness note to unlock today's session" flow with a small cohort; measure completion and return-next-day rate vs. free-access control.
- Missed because: it inverts the assumption that frictionless access maximizes usage — sometimes small effortful gates increase perceived value (IKEA effect).

**G. Sponsor-a-Session**
- Must hold: third-party financial/social stake (employer, partner) increases adherence without users feeling surveilled.
- Cheap falsifier: pilot with one B2B/EAP (employee assistance program) partner, compare adherence of sponsored vs. self-paid cohorts.
- Missed because: generalists think "referral/invite" (peer acquisition) not "third-party accountability" (retention) — different funnel stage entirely.

**H. Skipped Session Letter**
- Must hold: narrative/reflective continuity re-engages lapsed users better than guilt or streak-reset framing.
- Cheap falsifier: A/B the re-engagement email/in-app message for lapsed users: neutral streak-reset vs. reflective letter framing; measure return rate.
- Missed because: default lapse-recovery patterns are "we miss you" or streak-reset shame — reframing as narrative continuity is a copywriting-level insight most teams under-invest in.

**I. Commons Stewardship**
- Must hold: users care about a *shared* metric (cohort/neighborhood practice health) enough to act on behalf of the group, not just themselves.
- Cheap falsifier: show a test cohort a fake "community calm score" that dips, measure whether individual session-starts rise in response vs. control with only personal stats.
- Missed because: assumes individualism is the only lever; meditation communities (sanghas) have historically used shared commitment — this is a domain-specific insight generalists miss because they're optimizing individual psychology.

**J. Variable Session Reveal**
- Must hold: curiosity about *unknown content* drives opens more than user control/choice does.
- Cheap falsifier: A/B "surprise me" mode vs. full content picker, measure session-start rate and completion.
- Missed because: UX orthodoxy says "give users control," so concealment reads as bad design by default — but curiosity gaps are a well-documented distinct lever.

**K. Near-Miss Progress Bar**
- Must hold: biometric near-miss signals ethically motivate rather than manipulate.
- Cheap falsifier: N/A — this needs an ethics review before a usage test; the falsifier is really "would an ethicist/user researcher greenlight this," not a metrics test.
- **Flagged and deprioritized on ethical grounds** — structurally borrowed from slot-machine compulsion loops applied to a wellness product is a real tension (meditation app inducing compulsive-checking is arguably harmful to the product's own stated purpose). Keeping in the writeup as the "wildcard we rejected" is more honest than pretending it wasn't generated.

### Pairwise knockout (most likely to matter for THIS brief)

Round 1:
- A vs B → B (co-presence is lower-cost to test, less legally/financially complex than money-at-stake)
- C vs D → D (frictionless delivery addresses a bigger possible drop-off point, though it risks redefining DAU)
- E vs F → F (exchange market is more differentiated; state-triggers are closer to "smarter notifications," arguably a thin disguise of the banned mechanism)
- G vs H → G (third-party stake has larger scale potential — B2B channel — vs. a single re-engagement message)
- I vs J → I (commons stewardship taps a meditation-specific cultural truth; J is a generic curiosity-gap trick usable in any app)

Round 2:
- B vs D → B (D risks not counting as "DAU" at all under a literal reading of the brief; flag D as a wildcard/brief-challenge instead of a mainline pick)
- F vs G → G (G has clearer monetization/enterprise path and larger single-decision leverage — one employer partnership can move thousands of users)
- I vs (winner) → I vs G: I is more novel and harder to copy (competitors can't easily fake communal culture); G is more provable and revenue-adjacent

Final: **G (Sponsor-a-Session)** and **I (Commons Stewardship)** as top two; **B (Ambient Co-Presence)** as strong third; **D (Frictionless Delivery)** and **F (Exchange Market)** as wildcards.

# RECOMMENDATION

### Best practical idea: **G — Sponsor-a-Session**
**Non-obvious insight:** Retention economics change when a third party (employer, insurer, partner) has skin in the game — not through surveillance of *content*, but through visibility of *aggregate adherence*. This reframes DAU growth from a consumer-marketing problem into a B2B distribution problem, which most consumer-app teams don't consider because "growth" defaults to acquisition funnels, not accountability structures.
**First cheap test:** Pitch one existing corporate wellness/EAP partner (or even a single friendly employer) a pilot: they pre-pay for 50 seats, receive only an *aggregate* weekly adherence number, and employees know they're "sponsored." Compare 30-day adherence of this cohort against a matched self-pay cohort. If sponsored adherence isn't at least meaningfully higher, kill it before building any employer dashboard.

### Best wildcard idea: **I — Commons Stewardship**
**Non-obvious insight:** Meditation has a real cultural precedent (sangha/community practice) where individual return-to-practice is motivated by collective commitment, not personal reward — this is structurally different from social comparison (leaderboards) or social proof (testimonials), and it's borrowed legitimately from how contemplative communities have sustained practice for centuries, not decorated onto the app.
**First cheap test:** Add a single non-actionable UI element — a "collective calm" score for a random cohort's neighborhood/timezone group, updated daily, with zero other changes — and measure whether session-start rate moves on days the score dips, versus a control group shown only personal stats. No backend community features need to be built yet; the score can be semi-synthetic for the test.

**Second-best wildcard, flagged not recommended: K (Near-Miss Progress Bar)** — generated honestly through the borrowed-domain exercise, but excluded from recommendation because the mechanism (engineered near-miss on biometric data) risks manufacturing the exact compulsive-checking behavior a meditation product should be reducing. Included here so the ethical tradeoff is visible rather than silently dropped.
