## Two unrelated domains → structural transplant

**Coral reefs**
- *Symbiosis* → "adopt-a-local-business" subscription: retiree becomes a semi-permanent anchor patron/micro-investor in one café/shop, in exchange for status + perks
- *Bleaching (stress → symbiont expulsion)* → wellbeing-drift early warning: use disruption in routine/social contact — the "noise" of travel — as the actual signal for isolation or cognitive decline
- *Skeleton outlives the organism* → intergenerational compound: retirees fund a persistent shared property that houses the next cohort after they leave/die

**Air traffic control**
- *Separation minima* → real-time dynamic insurance buffer that auto-computes overlapping coverage as you cross borders, instead of static policy dates
- *Handoff protocol* → structured *live* doctor-to-doctor handoff calls for chronic conditions (not a data dump — an actual controlled handoff)
- *Flight plan filed in advance* → intention-based "life flight plan" + dead-man's switch: you file your itinerary/intentions; deviations trigger check-ins (consent-based, not GPS surveillance)

## Recommendations

**Best practical: E — Life Flight Plan + dead-man's switch**
The non-obvious move is treating the itinerary as a *filed intention* rather than a tracked location — it borrows ATC's logic that deviation-from-plan is what should trigger action, not continuous surveillance. This sidesteps the creepiness of Life360-style tracking (which retirees actively resent) while giving families genuine peace of mind. It's buildable fast (workflow + notifications, no medical/legal/real-estate entanglement), the assumption set is thin (people will pre-declare plans if it clearly serves *their* autonomy, not their family's control), and it directly answers the top fear this demographic actually has: dying or getting stuck alone somewhere no one's watching.

**Best wildcard: L — Intergenerational compound**
The insight worth taking seriously even though it's slow and legally messy: retirement currently treats "legacy" as money left to heirs after you're gone. This reframes legacy as *infrastructure you build while you're still around to enjoy it* — a physical, persistent community asset (like a coral skeleton) that houses the next wave of nomad-retirees after you move on or die. It converts an existential problem (what's the point of accumulating wealth if you have no fixed home) into a community-building product. High risk, but if even a boutique version works, it creates a genuinely new asset class rather than another app in a crowded travel-tech space.

**Honorable mention (dark-horse #2): B — Wellbeing-drift detection.** Same impact ceiling as L, but riskier on the product-trust side rather than the legal side — worth prototyping narrowly (e.g., opt-in check-in-pattern alerts for one condition, like early dementia) before generalizing.
