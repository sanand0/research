## BAN

Ideas a smart generalist lists in 30 seconds — banned, along with disguised variants (comic adaptations, "smart" hyperlinked ebooks, badge/streak systems, Q&A chatbots-on-text, multi-voice podcast recaps):

1. Audiobooks / narration
2. Summary apps (Blinkist-style)
3. Interactive/hyperlinked ebooks
4. AI chatbot you quiz about the book
5. Graphic novel adaptation
6. Podcast-style discussion of chapters
7. Gamified reading (streaks, badges, social challenges)

No later idea may rely on "compress it," "narrate it," "add clickable enrichment," "chat with a bot about it," "redraw it visually," or "add points" as its core mechanism.

## DIVERGE

**Dimensions:** mechanism of intake (visual/auditory/tactile/spatial/embodied) · actor (solo/group/hybrid-with-machine/proxy) · who pays and how (money/labor/disclosure/inheritance) · timing (linear/dilated/reordered/event-triggered) · what's removed (the author/order/solitude/the text itself) · what's inverted (reader↔writer, book reads you, permanent↔ephemeral)

**Borrowed domains** (rules must be load-bearing, not decorative):

*Ecology (decomposition/succession):* nutrients need decay before reuse → pioneer species enable and are destroyed by later stages → keystone species control the whole system disproportionately.
*Contract law:* no binding deal without mutual consideration → terms fixed except at pre-agreed break clauses → inaction can legally count as acceptance.

## CONVERGE

**Critiques of the seven strongest survivors:**

- **Decomposer Reading** — must-hold assumption: forced forgetting produces deeper integration, not just abandonment. Cheap kill-test: 10-person trial with mandatory 3-day chapter locks vs. free-pacing control, compare completion + recall quality. Missed by generalists because they optimize reading tools for retention (repetition), never for engineered loss.
- **Keystone Reader Edition** — assumption: readers accept structurally unequal access as meaningful rather than as being cheated. Test: rotate the "keystone" role weekly in a 5-person group, survey satisfaction. Missed because default mental model of "social reading" is equal access for all.
- **Consideration Reading** — assumption: people will supply real disclosure/effort rather than gaming the gate with junk. Test: require one paragraph of personal writing before unlocking each chapter, watch submission quality over 20 users. Missed because "payment for content" defaults to money, not reciprocal specific performance.
- **Geographic Book** — assumption: enough readers will physically travel to fixed coordinates. Test: lock one chapter to a real landmark, measure completion vs. abandonment. Missed because location is normally decorative (a map), never a hard gate.
- **The Unread Book** — assumption: readers want reading's *timing* removed from their own control. Test: 10 users get passages pushed only when calendar/location matches a plot beat; compare felt connection to direct readers. Missed because it looks adjacent to the banned "AI summarizer" but the actual inversion (book decides when, not you) isn't in that frame.
- **Somatic Transcription** — assumption: meaning can be encoded/recovered through movement alone, no language channel. Test: encode one paragraph as 10 gestures, 5 people perform and freewrite the meaning, check for above-chance convergence vs. a normal-text control group. Missed because it questions whether the result still counts as "reading" at all — too uncomfortable a boundary for default ideation.
- **Marginalia Relay** — assumption: sequential strangers' notes accumulate into value, not noise. Test: pass one copy through 10 people, one mandatory annotation each, have an 11th person compare engagement against a clean copy. Missed because existing social reading keeps annotation optional and non-sequential; mandatory dependency is the twist.

**Pairwise knockouts** ("most likely to matter for this brief"):
- Decomposer Reading beats Keystone Reader Edition (works for solo readers — the majority case — and is cleaner to prototype).
- Geographic Book beats Consideration Reading (more viscerally redefines the *act* of reading, not just its economics).
- Somatic Transcription beats The Unread Book (goes further — questions whether text/language is even required).
- Final: Decomposer Reading vs. Somatic Transcription — rather than eliminate one, they turn out to answer different questions the brief implicitly asks (practical vs. radical), so both survive to the recommendation.

## Recommendation

**Best practical idea — Decomposer Reading.** Non-obvious insight: retention tools assume more contact = better; this bets the opposite — that forgetting is a *necessary processing step*, not a failure, and blocking re-access forces real transformation instead of rehearsal. Buildable today as an ebook-app feature (lock chapter N+1 until a self-report or light quiz shows chapter N has genuinely faded). First cheap test: 10 readers, mandatory 72-hour gaps, compare depth of recall/insight against free-pacing controls.

**Best wildcard idea — Somatic Transcription.** Non-obvious insight: every format on the list still assumes reading needs a linguistic channel (visual or auditory); this asks whether meaning can travel through the body alone, with muscle memory standing in for the text. First cheap test: encode one paragraph as a 10-move gesture sequence, have people "read" it by performing the moves and freewrite what they think it meant, then check for shared meaning above chance against people who just read the paragraph normally.
