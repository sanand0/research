Do not choose only one action. Use a combined plan.

**Recommended action**

Cut noncritical costs now. Start fundraising now. Continue growth only in customer groups with good retention.

Six months of runway is a high risk. Fundraising can take several months. The increase in churn can make revenue growth temporary.

**Key trade-offs**

**Cut costs**

Benefit: The company gets more time to fix churn and raise capital.

Risk: Large cuts can reduce product quality, sales capacity, and growth.

Best use: Remove low-value work, slow hiring, and reduce optional spending. Do not cut teams that directly improve retention.

**Raise now**

Benefit: The company can raise before cash becomes critical.

Risk: Investors can use the short runway and higher churn to reduce the valuation. They can also delay the process until retention improves.

Best use: Start investor discussions now. Show a clear plan to find the cause of churn and control cash use.

**Keep growing**

Benefit: Fifteen percent monthly growth can support a strong investment case.

Risk: Growth can hide a retention problem. The company can spend cash to replace customers that leave.

Best use: Continue growth only where customer lifetime value is clearly higher than acquisition cost.

**Important checks**

Measure churn by customer group, product, price plan, and acquisition channel.

Check whether churn doubled because of one large customer, a product problem, a price change, or poor customer quality.

Track net revenue retention, gross margin, customer acquisition cost, payback period, and burn multiple.

**What would change the answer**

Cut costs more strongly when runway is less than six months, fundraising conditions are weak, or churn affects most customer groups.

Focus on fundraising when net revenue retention is stable, gross margins are good, and the company can show a credible path to 12 or more months of runway.

Keep growing faster when churn is temporary, unit economics are strong, and new customers have better retention than old customers.

Stop aggressive growth when customer acquisition payback is longer than the remaining runway or when each new customer increases cash loss.

The main rule is simple: Do not use growth to hide churn. Fix retention, extend runway, and raise capital at the same time.
