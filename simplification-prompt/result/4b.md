## Experiment

Use a randomized, within-person crossover test.

Recruit at least 40 employees. Use real employees from the target role. Give each employee 20 representative decision cases.

For each employee:

* Assign 10 cases to the AI condition.
* Assign 10 matched cases to the control condition.
* Randomize the case order.
* Do not tell the employee which cases affect the main test.

In the AI condition, show the AI suggestion before the final decision.

In the control condition, show the same interface without a suggestion. Give the employee access to the normal tools and data.

Do not let employees use other AI tools during the test.

## Decision cases

Use cases that have a known outcome or a reference decision.

Include easy, medium, and difficult cases. Match the cases across the two conditions.

Do not use trivia questions. Use decisions that represent the employee's normal work.

Record the employee's first answer and final answer. This data shows when the AI changes a decision.

## Primary metric

Use decision quality as the primary metric.

Independent experts must score each final decision. The experts must not know the test condition.

Use a fixed scoring guide. For example:

* Correct result: 0 to 4 points.
* Evidence quality: 0 to 3 points.
* Risk identification: 0 to 2 points.
* Explanation quality: 0 to 1 point.

The total score is 0 to 10.

Measure the mean paired score difference between the AI and control conditions.

Also measure the rate of serious errors. A serious error is a decision that can cause material cost, harm, or policy failure.

## Secondary metrics

Measure these items separately:

* Time to final decision.
* Confidence before and after the suggestion.
* Rate of answer changes.
* Rate of correct changes.
* Rate of incorrect changes.
* Agreement between expert scorers.

Do not combine speed and quality into one primary metric. A faster bad decision is not an improvement.

## Main analysis

Compare each employee with the same employee.

Use a mixed-effects model. Include employee and case as random effects. Include condition, case difficulty, and order as fixed effects.

Report:

* Mean quality difference.
* 95 percent confidence interval.
* Serious-error difference.
* Time difference.
* Results by case difficulty.

The AI improves decision quality only when the quality result improves. A time reduction alone is not success.

## Main confounds

Control these confounds:

* **Case difficulty:** Randomize matched cases.
* **Employee skill:** Use the within-person design.
* **Learning:** Use different but equivalent cases.
* **Order effects:** Randomize the condition order.
* **Expert bias:** Hide the condition from the scorers.
* **AI output variation:** Store and audit all suggestions.
* **Automation bias:** Measure incorrect answer changes.
* **Extra information:** Keep all non-AI information equal.
* **Contamination:** Prevent AI use in the control condition.
* **Novelty:** Give a short practice session before measurement.
* **Unequal effort:** Record time, clicks, and missing answers.

## Stopping rule

Define a useful improvement before the test starts. Use 0.20 standard deviations in the quality score as the minimum useful effect.

Use this fixed rule:

1. Complete data collection for at least 40 employees.
2. Do not inspect condition results before data collection ends.
3. Declare success only when all conditions are true:

   * The estimated quality gain is at least 0.20 standard deviations.
   * The lower limit of the 95 percent confidence interval is above zero.
   * The serious-error rate does not increase.
   * The improvement appears in more than one difficulty level.
4. Declare no useful effect when the upper limit of the confidence interval is below 0.20 standard deviations.
5. Otherwise, report the result as uncertain. Do not report success.

This design is small because each employee supplies both control and AI data. It is reliable because it measures quality with blind scoring and matched work cases.
