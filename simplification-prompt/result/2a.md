The key normalization is:

* Customer volume: **+10%**
* Tickets: **+40%**
* Therefore, tickets per customer rose by **1.40 / 1.10 − 1 = 27%**
* Resolution time: **−20%**

So this is not merely growth. The chatbot changed either contact behavior, ticket creation, ticket mix, or measurement.

| Plausible cause                                        | Evidence that would distinguish it                                                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The bot fails, then escalates users**                | High ticket creation after low-confidence answers, repeated questions, “didn’t help” clicks, or abandonment. Bot-to-agent escalation rate and tickets per bot session rise.     |
| **The bot makes support easier to access**             | More tickets from customers who previously never contacted support; growth concentrated in low-severity questions. CSAT may remain stable or improve despite higher volume.     |
| **Duplicate ticket creation**                          | The same customer and issue generates a bot transcript plus an agent ticket, or multiple tickets within a short interval. Unique incidents rise much less than ticket IDs.      |
| **The bot actively encourages escalation**             | Ticket growth clusters around particular intents, conversation branches, or calls to action such as “contact support.” Small wording changes materially alter escalation rates. |
| **The bot creates new confusion or product problems**  | Tickets mention incorrect bot advice, contradictory instructions, failed actions, or chatbot-specific complaints. Product usage errors rise after affected conversations.       |
| **A simultaneous external change caused the increase** | The rise is concentrated by product version, geography, campaign, customer segment, or incident—not by chatbot exposure. Unexposed customers show a similar increase.           |
| **Ticket classification or logging changed**           | Previously unrecorded chats are now counted as tickets, or tickets are split differently. Operational workload, unique cases, or agent hours do not rise by 40%.                |
| **The case mix became simpler**                        | More low-complexity tickets explain both higher count and lower average resolution time. Median and within-intent resolution times may be unchanged.                            |
| **Agents are closing cases prematurely**               | Resolution time falls, but reopen rates, repeat-contact rates, transfers, complaints, or low CSAT rise.                                                                         |
| **The chatbot pre-collects useful context**            | Resolution time falls within the same issue types and severity levels, without higher reopen rates. Tickets may be up, but agent effort per resolved customer problem is down.  |
| **Simpson’s paradox or metric distortion**             | Overall resolution time falls because the proportion of easy tickets increases, while resolution time within major categories stays flat or worsens.                            |

### Best next action

Run a **customer-level randomized holdout** for one to two weeks: keep a small representative group on the previous support experience and compare it with chatbot-exposed customers.

Use the customer or incident—not raw ticket count—as the unit of analysis. Measure:

* unique support incidents per 1,000 customers;
* tickets per incident and duplicate rate;
* bot containment and escalation by intent;
* total time to durable resolution, including bot time;
* agent minutes per incident;
* reopen and repeat-contact rates;
* CSAT and unresolved-problem rate.

At the same time, trace every new ticket back to its chatbot session and inspect the few intents contributing most of the increase.

The decision rule is straightforward:

* **More tickets but fewer agent minutes, equal-or-better durable resolution and CSAT:** likely improved accessibility; retain the chatbot and optimize capacity.
* **More repeat contacts, duplicates, reopens, or low-confidence escalations:** repair those flows and ticket-creation logic.
* **The holdout also rises:** investigate external demand or measurement changes rather than blaming the chatbot.

Do not optimize on average resolution time alone. The primary outcome should be **cost and customer effort per durably resolved incident**.
