01 / THE SHORT ANSWER

Singapore passes the Japan test—carefully.

In 2024, Singapore recorded 0.18 severe delays per million train-km. Tokyo Metro recorded 0.26; Nagoya recorded none. In the latest punctuality comparison, Singapore sat at 99.29% and Tokyo Metro at 98.00%.

But the clocks and definitions differ across cities. This is evidence that Singapore belongs among reliable metro systems—not proof that every Singapore train shadows a Japanese timetable.

Reliability is high. It is not perfectly flat.

Annual end-of-line punctuality stayed between 99.35% and 99.65% network-wide from 2020–2025. Line-level variation is more useful than that calm headline.

05 / COMMUTER RULES

Three habits beat one reassuring number.

  1. 01

    Budget the transfer, not the train.

    The official score is end-to-end. Walking, interchange queues and an irregular gap can dominate a short journey.

  2. 02

    Check alerts when lateness is costly.

    System reliability is excellent, but the rare disruption can affect tens of thousands at once. Aggregate history cannot tell you what is happening now.

  3. 03

    Read “99%” with its fine print.

    Punctuality means within two minutes at a terminal or turnaround. Service delivery means scheduled distance operated. Neither is your platform wait.
